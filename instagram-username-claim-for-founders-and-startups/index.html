Instagram username claim for founders and startups.

You have the domain and the company, but the Instagram handle is held by someone else. Here is how founders can go after it through Instagram’s official routes.

Short answer

Founders can’t buy an Instagram handle, but there are official routes: an inactive or abandoned account, an impersonator of your brand, or a registered trademark that matches the name. Company registration alone doesn’t give you a right to a handle. Unbanz sends the claim through those routes, and you get a 100% refund if it fails.

Routes that can work

  • Trademark: a registered trademark that matches the handle is the strongest route, through Meta’s intellectual property report. Have the registration number and office ready (USPTO, UKIPO, EUIPO, CIPO, IP Australia). See trademark username claims.
  • Inactive or abandoned account: no posts, no photo, no activity for a long time. There’s no “inactive” button, so the claim goes through the platform’s own processes.
  • Impersonation: an account posing as your business. Instagram’s impersonation report applies.
  • Squatter: an account holding your name to sell it back. Paying them breaks Instagram’s terms.

What usually doesn’t work

  • A company registration or domain on its own. It doesn’t give you a right to a handle.
  • The handle is an active account run by someone unrelated.
  • A trademark that covers a different industry, with no confusion between the two.

Plan it before you launch

  • Check the handle on Instagram before you commit to a brand name or print anything.
  • Create your account early, even before launch, so a clean handle doesn’t go to someone else.
  • Choose a backup: yourbrandhq, getyourbrand, yourbrand.co. Avoid stacked underscores and numbers.
  • Think about a trademark early. It’s the clearest way to show a brand name is yours, and it helps across platforms.
