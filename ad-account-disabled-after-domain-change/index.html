Ad account disabled after changing your domain or business name?

A rebrand or a new website is a normal business event, but it can look unusual to Meta. This page is for advertisers who changed a name or domain and then lost their ad account.

Updated 29 October 2026

Message us on WhatsApp ↗︎Prefer email? ↗︎

A sudden new domain, brand or Page name can look like an attempt to start over after a restriction. Verify the domain, make the website and business details match, and request a review that explains the change plainly. Unbanz can prepare the appeal and supporting documents.

Verify the domain

Domain verification ties your site to your portfolio.

Match the details

Legal name, site and Page name should be consistent.

Explain the change

A short explanation with proof, for example a registration update, is enough.

Why a change triggers a check

  • The ad account was restricted before and the new domain looks like a restart.
  • The new site has the same products and layout as a restricted one.
  • The landing page changed after the ad was approved.
  • The Page name changed several times in a short period.

What to do

  1. Verify the new domain in business settings.
  2. Update the Page name only through Meta’s official change process.
  3. Make sure the website shows the same legal name and contact details.
  4. Pause ads that point to the old domain or pages that no longer exist.
  5. Request the review and attach proof of the change, such as a registration document or invoice.

What not to do

Do not move ads to a clean domain to avoid a review. Reviewers can connect them, and it looks like what circumventing notices describe. See our page on circumventing systems notices.

How Unbanz helps

We build a short, clear timeline of the change and write the appeal around it. Read the guide to domain verification too.

What to send

  • The ad account ID and the business portfolio ID.
  • A screenshot of the notice in Account Quality, with the date it appeared.
  • What was running: the product or offer, the landing page and roughly how much was being spent per day.
  • Anything that changed shortly before: a new card, a new admin, a new domain, a login from another country.

Please do not send passwords, login codes, card numbers or access tokens.

Can changing my domain get my ad account disabled?+

It can lead to extra checks, especially if the account has a restricted history.

Do I need to verify the new domain?+

It is recommended and required for some features. It helps tie the site to your business.

Should I keep the old domain live?+

Keep it live with a redirect where possible, so ads and customers do not hit dead pages.

Will Meta accept a rebrand?+

A real rebrand with proof is normal. Meta decides each review.
