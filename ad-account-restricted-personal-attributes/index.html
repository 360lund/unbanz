Ad account restricted for personal attributes?

Ads that speak to a person as if you know something private about them are rejected. This page is for coaches, clinics, fitness and dating-adjacent advertisers who got that notice.

Updated 29 October 2026

Message us on WhatsApp ↗︎Prefer email? ↗︎

Meta restricts ads that assert or imply a personal attribute, such as health, finances, race or relationship status. Rewrite the ad so it talks about the offer, not the viewer, then request a review in Account Quality. Unbanz can prepare the account appeal if rejections keep adding up.

What it means

Wording like “Are you struggling with…” suggests you know a private fact about the reader.

How to rewrite

Describe the product or service and who it is for in general terms, without addressing the person’s condition.

Then request review

Fix the creative and landing page, then ask for a review of the ad account.

Examples of the difference

  • Flagged: “Do you have diabetes? Try this.” Safer: “A guide to meal planning.”
  • Flagged: “Are you broke?” Safer: “Free budgeting workshop.”
  • Flagged: “Lonely at 40?” Safer: “Meet-ups for adults in Leeds.”
  • Flagged: before and after photos that suggest a medical result.

Where to check

Read the ad text, the headline, the image text and the landing page. Meta checks all of them. Targeting must also respect the policy, and some sensitive categories have limited targeting options. Our guide on health, wellness and personal attributes goes through more examples.

Steps

  1. Screenshot the rejection and the policy it names.
  2. Rewrite headlines and body text so they do not address the reader’s condition or situation.
  3. Remove before and after images and any claim of a result.
  4. Check the landing page for the same wording.
  5. Request the review, and if the account itself is restricted, request that too.

How Unbanz helps

We go through the ads and the landing page against the policy, show you what to change, and prepare the appeal for the account. We can also help you describe your business honestly in the appeal. See ads for health and wellness businesses.

What to send

  • The ad account ID and the business portfolio ID.
  • A screenshot of the notice in Account Quality, with the date it appeared.
  • What was running: the product or offer, the landing page and roughly how much was being spent per day.
  • Anything that changed shortly before: a new card, a new admin, a new domain, a login from another country.

Please do not send passwords, login codes, card numbers or access tokens.

Good to know.

What counts as a personal attribute?+

Examples are health, disability, financial status, religion, race, sexuality, age and relationship status, when the ad suggests it applies to the reader.

Can I still advertise a clinic or a coaching offer?+

Yes, as long as the ad describes the service without implying private facts about the viewer and follows the other policies.

Will a rewrite alone lift the restriction?+

It fixes future rejections. An account that is already restricted still needs a review.

Is it allowed to use testimonials?+

Real testimonials can be fine, but results claims and before and after images are often rejected.

Do I need to share my Facebook or Meta login?+

No. Your password and login codes stay with you. Unbanz works from the ad account ID, the portfolio ID and a screenshot of the notice. Anyone who asks for your login is not someone to work with.
