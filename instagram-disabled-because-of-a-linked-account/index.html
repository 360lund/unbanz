Disabled because of an account you do not recognise.

When Instagram says your account was disabled because it is connected to another account that broke the rules, and you have never heard of that account.

Updated 5 November 2026

Message us on WhatsApp ↗︎Prefer email? ↗︎

“Linked” usually means Meta saw signals tying the two accounts together, such as the same device, phone number, email, IP address or Accounts Center profile. It does not need to be an account you own. Shared phones, a family Wi-Fi, a reused phone number or a hacker who logged in from your device can all create a link. Appeal from the notice and explain plainly that you do not control the other account and why the signals might overlap. Meta makes the final decision.

Shared signals

Device, phone, email or network.

Say it plainly

You do not control the other account.

Clean up

Remove unknown logins and devices.

Common reasons two accounts look linked

  • A phone, laptop or tablet used by more than one person.
  • A phone number that used to belong to someone else.
  • The same home, office, school or hostel Wi-Fi.
  • An account added to your Accounts Center, or an old account you forgot.
  • A takeover: the person who hacked you logged in to their own accounts from your session.

What to write in the appeal

Name your own account and what you use it for. Say that you do not run the other account and do not know who does. Give the likely reason for the overlap in one sentence, for example a shared family phone or a recycled number. Mention any security problem you noticed. Do not guess at the other person’s identity or accuse anyone.

Clean up before and after

  1. In Accounts Center, remove any profile you do not recognise.
  2. Check where you are logged in and log out of unknown devices.
  3. Change your password and turn on two-factor with an authenticator app.
  4. Stop using shared devices for the account while the appeal is open.

Before you open any link

  1. Screenshot the full notice, including the date and any reference number.
  2. Use the phone or computer you usually log in from, on your normal network, without a VPN.
  3. Appeal once, in plain words. Repeated or copy-pasted appeals do not help.
  4. Do not make a new account while you wait.
  5. Never pay anyone for a “private” or “direct” appeal link. There is none.

Good to know.

Can I be disabled for someone else’s account?+

Yes. If Meta’s systems connect your account to one that broke the rules, both can be actioned. You can explain the overlap in the appeal.

Should I mention the other username?+

Only if the notice names it. Say you do not control it.

Will removing the link restore my account?+

Removing unknown links and devices is good practice, but only the appeal can reverse a disable.

Is my password needed for help?+

No. Your password and login codes stay with you.
