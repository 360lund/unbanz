What to write in a TikTok appeal.

A short structure that works for most bans, with examples.

Updated 11 November 2026

Message us on WhatsApp ↗︎Prefer email? ↗︎

A good TikTok appeal is three to five sentences: identify the account, state that it is yours, explain briefly what happened, acknowledge the rules, and ask for a review. Avoid anger, long stories, and copy-paste templates found online. One clear appeal is better than several.

Short

Under 100 words.

Factual

Dates and what you posted.

Calm

No threats or blame.

Structure

  1. Who you are and the username.
  2. That you own the account (email or phone on it).
  3. What you think happened, in one sentence.
  4. That you have read the Community Guidelines.
  5. A request to review the decision.

Example wording

  • Mistaken ban: “I run @name for my bakery. I believe my account was banned by mistake, possibly after a video was reported. I follow the Community Guidelines. Please review the ban.”
  • Spam flag: “I used a follower app last week without realising it broke the rules. I have removed it. Please review my account.”
  • Hacked first: “My account was hacked on [date] and the hacker posted content that is not mine. I have since secured my email. Please restore the account.”

Mistakes that get appeals rejected

  • Threatening legal action.
  • Blaming other creators.
  • Sending the same text many times.
  • Admitting to things you did not do because a template said so.
