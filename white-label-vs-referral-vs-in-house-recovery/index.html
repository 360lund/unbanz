White label vs referral vs in-house account recovery.

Three ways to deal with a client’s disabled account. Here is how they differ and which one suits which agency.

Message us on WhatsApp ↗︎Prefer email? ↗︎

An agency can handle a client’s disabled account in three ways. In-house means your own team does the appeals. A referral sends the client to another company. White label keeps the client with you while a specialist works behind the scenes. White label keeps the relationship without building the know-how.

In-house

Your people do everything. Most control, most training, and it only pays off if lockouts are frequent.

Referral

You pass the client on. Least work for you, but the client now has a relationship with someone else.

White label

You keep the client and a specialist does the platform work. A middle path.

In-house: what it really asks of you

Doing it yourself means learning the appeal route for each platform and problem, keeping up as menus and rules change, and absorbing the time when a request is rejected and needs a second approach. If you have a person who enjoys that work and your volume is steady, it can be the right call. The detail is in white label vs in-house.

Referral: simple, with a catch

A referral costs you nothing to set up, and it feels honest. The catch is that your client now knows another company, and some will keep using it. If your agency sells retainers, that is a real cost. See white label vs freelancer vs referral for the freelancer angle.

White label: what you give up

You give up a little margin to the partner and you rely on them to be honest about what is possible. In return you keep the client, the relationship and the voice. It does not remove the platform’s authority: Meta or the platform concerned still decides.

Choosing between them

  • Lockouts every month and a trained person in the team: consider in-house.
  • Lockouts a few times a year and you want to keep clients: white label.
  • Almost never: a referral, or a clear note in your terms.

QUICK ANSWERS

Good to know.

What is the difference between white label and a referral?+

In a referral the client is handed to another company and deals with it. In white label the client stays with the agency and never deals with the specialist.

Is in-house recovery cheaper than white label?+

It depends on volume. In-house needs a trained person and time to keep up with platform changes. For occasional lockouts, white label can cost less than building that skill.

Can I mix the options?+

Yes. Some agencies handle simple lockouts themselves and send the rest to a white label partner. Others refer clients only for platforms they do not cover.

Does white label change who decides the outcome?+

No. Meta or the platform concerned decides in every option, and no provider can promise an outcome.

Which option protects client relationships best?+

In-house and white label both keep the client with you. A referral puts another company in front of your client.
