Signs your account was hacked

  • An email from Instagram saying your email, phone number or password changed, and you didn’t do it.
  • Your login stops working, or the account shows a different email.
  • Posts, DMs or follows you didn’t make, or a changed username and bio.
  • Login codes arriving that you didn’t request.

What to do, in order

  1. Find Instagram’s email about the change (check spam). If it offers a way to revert or secure the account, use it at once. It expires.
  2. Go to instagram.com/hacked on a phone or computer you’ve used to log in before, on your usual network.
  3. Choose the option that matches what you still have: the old email, the old number, or neither. Instagram can offer a video selfie where you turn your head in different directions.
  4. Once you’re back in, change the password, then remove any email or number you don’t recognise.
  5. Open Accounts Center, then Password and security, and log out of sessions you don’t know. Turn on two-factor authentication with an authenticator app rather than SMS if you can.

Secure your email first

The hacker usually got in through your email or a phishing page. If your email password is the same, or it’s also compromised, they can undo your recovery. Change the email password and turn on two-step verification there before you rely on Instagram’s codes. See also when a hacker changed your email and password.

If the account is also disabled

Hackers often post spam or scams, and that can get the account disabled. If you see a disabled notice, tell Instagram in the review that the account was compromised, and finish the selfie check. Details: appealing a disabled account after a hack.

What not to do

  • Ignore “hackers” who contact you first, and never give anyone your password.
  • Don’t click links in emails that claim to be from Instagram unless you can see they come from Instagram’s own domain.
  • Don’t create a new account and abandon the old one before you’ve tried the official routes.
