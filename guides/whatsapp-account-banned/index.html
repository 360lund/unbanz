WhatsApp account banned: why it happens and how to request a review

You open WhatsApp and see “This phone number can't use WhatsApp.” Your chats, groups and contacts are suddenly out of reach. Here is what WhatsApp says about bans, how the review works, and the special cases of unofficial apps and second-hand phone numbers.

What the ban message means

WhatsApp's About account bans page says a banned account shows: “This phone number can't use WhatsApp: [phone number]. You can request a review of this number. Most reviews are completed within 24 hours.”

WhatsApp bans accounts that it believes violate its Terms of Service. The “Acceptable use” section of the terms covers things like spam, scams, automated or bulk messaging, and activity that puts other users' safety at risk.

If you use the WhatsApp Business app or the API, read our WhatsApp Business ban guide, because the review routes are different.

How to request a review

  1. Open WhatsApp on the phone with the banned number.
  2. Tap Request review.
  3. Complete any steps shown, such as entering a code sent by SMS.
  4. Wait for the WhatsApp notification with the decision. You can check the status by opening WhatsApp.

WhatsApp notes three things worth knowing:

  • It reviews one phone number per appeal.
  • If you don't see an option to request a review, the ban is final and can't be appealed.
  • Contacting WhatsApp outside the review process, or through another person's account, won't speed it up or change the decision.

It also says third-party services can't ban your WhatsApp account or remove a ban.

Special cases

You used an unofficial app

WhatsApp's unofficial apps page says that to keep using WhatsApp you need to uninstall the unofficial app and download the official WhatsApp or WhatsApp Business app from its website or an official app store. Modified versions with extra features, such as auto-reply, hidden ticks or bulk sending, are a common cause of bans. Back up what you can before switching, because unofficial apps' backups may not transfer.

Your number used to belong to someone else

WhatsApp's previously owned numbers page says that if you're using a new phone number, it's possible the previous owner violated the Terms of Service and that caused the ban. You can tap Request review and submit the request; once reviewed, you'll receive a notification. After an unban, you verify the number again to access the account.

A temporary ban

WhatsApp also has temporary bans, often linked to unofficial apps or unusual activity. Follow the on-screen countdown and instructions rather than repeatedly reinstalling.

Common reasons for bans

In what we see:

  • sending many messages to people who don't have your number saved;
  • adding people to groups without their permission;
  • being blocked or reported by many people in a short period;
  • using automation, bulk-sending tools or modified apps;
  • sharing scams, malware links or illegal content.

After an unban

WhatsApp has a separate after your account is unbanned page. Once you're back, change the behaviour that probably caused the ban. Check your groups, stop forwarding chain messages to people who don't know you, and remove any unofficial apps.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Reinstalling over and over. The ban is on the number, not the phone.
  • Moving straight to a new number and repeating the same activity. It is likely to be banned too.
  • Paying an “unban service” that asks for your SMS code. That code is how someone takes over your WhatsApp. Never share it.

Before you request a review

WhatsApp's review is short, so prepare before you tap the button. Write down what you used the number for, roughly when the ban started, and anything unusual you did in the days before: joining or creating large groups, forwarding the same message to many people, installing a new app or changing phones. If you used a modified app, uninstall it and install the official app from your app store first. If the number is new to you, note the date you got the SIM. You won't always get a text box, but knowing the likely cause helps you avoid repeating it after an unban, which matters because repeat bans are much harder to reverse.

Protect your chats for next time

Turn on chat backup to Google Drive or iCloud in WhatsApp's settings. A ban can't be undone by a backup, but if your account is restored, or you must start again on a new number, a recent backup means you lose far less.

YOUR QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

How long does a WhatsApp ban review take?+

WhatsApp's ban message says most reviews are completed within 24 hours. You'll get a WhatsApp notification with the decision.

Can I appeal if there's no Request review button?+

WhatsApp says that if you don't see an option to request a review, the ban is final and can't be appealed.

My new SIM card's number is banned. What do I do?+

WhatsApp says the previous owner may have caused the ban. Tap Request review in the app to ask WhatsApp to review the number.

Will I lose my chats if my WhatsApp is banned?+

You can't access them while the account is banned. If the ban is lifted, you verify the number and continue. Keeping regular backups is the best protection.
