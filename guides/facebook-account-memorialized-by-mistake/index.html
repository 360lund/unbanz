Facebook account memorialized by mistake: how to get it back

You try to log in and your profile now says “Remembering” next to your name. Friends are sending condolences. It is a distressing mistake, but there is a specific official route to undo it. Here is how memorialization works and how to get your account back.

What memorialization is

Facebook's memorialization help page describes memorialized accounts as a place for friends and family to share memories after someone has passed away, and says memorializing an account “helps keep it secure by preventing anyone from logging into it.” Facebook's policy is to memorialize an account when it receives a valid request.

That is why a mistaken memorialization locks you out: the account is put into a special state where normal login is blocked.

How it happens by mistake

In the cases we see, it is usually one of these:

  • A confused report. Someone reports the wrong profile, for example a person with the same name who has passed away.
  • A malicious report. Someone files a memorialization request to get you locked out, sometimes using an obituary for someone else.
  • A system error. Facebook has, in the past, briefly shown memorial banners on living people's profiles because of a bug, which it later apologised for.

The official route: “My personal account is memorialized”

Facebook has a dedicated form for this, titled My Personal Account is Memorialized. It says: “If you believe we've mistakenly memorialized your account, please let us know.” Use it rather than a disabled-account appeal, unless Facebook also shows a separate suspended or disabled notice.

The form asks for:

  • your full name and a contact email address that works;
  • a photo of yourself (a selfie) holding your government-issued ID, so Facebook can confirm you are the owner;
  • if you have no government ID, a photo of yourself holding two forms of ID, one of which includes a photo or date of birth matching your profile.

Facebook says your face must be clearly visible in both the photo and the ID. Check which documents count on Facebook's accepted ID page.

Step by step

  1. Screenshot the profile showing “Remembering” and any login message you see.
  2. Prepare the ID photo. Use a valid, unexpired ID in the same name as the profile. Take the selfie in good light, holding the ID next to your face, with all text readable.
  3. Open the form in a browser and fill it in carefully. Use an email address you check daily, ideally the one on the account.
  4. Explain in one or two sentences: “I am the owner of this account and I am alive. It was memorialized by mistake. Please remove the memorialized status and restore my access.”
  5. Submit once and watch your email, including spam, for messages from Facebook or Meta domains.
  6. Tell close contacts directly by phone or another app that you are fine, so the condolence messages stop.

If your profile name doesn't match your ID

This is the most common reason these requests stall. If you use a nickname or a different spelling on Facebook, include an ID that shows the name you use, or two IDs that together link your legal name and profile name. Facebook's accepted ID list includes non-government documents for this reason.

If you think someone did it on purpose

Write down who might have done it and when. Once you are back in, turn on two-factor authentication, review your privacy settings and consider who can see your posts. If you are being harassed, report the person through Facebook and, where appropriate, to the police.

While you wait

  • Do not open a new Facebook profile to replace it. Facebook allows one personal profile per person, and a duplicate can complicate the case.
  • If you manage Pages or ad accounts, ask a co-admin to keep them running. Facebook's older help notes that Pages with a sole admin whose account was memorialized may be removed after a valid memorialization request, which is another reason every Page should have a second admin.

How long it takes

Facebook does not publish a fixed timeframe for reversing a mistaken memorialization. Some people are back in within days; others wait longer, particularly when the ID name doesn't match the profile. Avoid sending the form repeatedly, which can create duplicate cases.

YOUR QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

Can a memorialized Facebook account be restored?+

Yes, if it was memorialized by mistake. Facebook has a dedicated form for living account owners that asks for a selfie holding your government-issued ID.

What if I don't have a government ID?+

Facebook's form says you can take a photo of yourself holding two forms of ID, one of which must include a photo or date of birth matching your profile.

Can my family log in to a memorialized account?+

No. Facebook says it can't provide login information for someone else's account, even in these circumstances.

Should I use the disabled account appeal instead?+

Only if Facebook also shows that your account is suspended or disabled. For mistaken memorialization, use the specific memorialized-account form.
