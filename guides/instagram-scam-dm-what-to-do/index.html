Got a scam DM on Instagram? What to do before and after you click

Short answer: don’t click, reply or pay. Report the message, block the account and save evidence. If you already paid or shared a password, act within the hour on your bank and your login.

Scam DMs have a few recurring shapes. Recognising them takes seconds, and the right response after a mistake limits the damage a lot.

What to do first

Stop. Don’t reply, don’t click and don’t send money, codes or ID. Take a screenshot of the message and the profile. If it came from a friend’s account, that account may be hacked, so contact the friend another way.

Step 1: Recognise the common shapes

  • “Your account will be banned” messages with a link to appeal or verify.
  • Giveaway and prize messages that need a fee or details first.
  • Fake brand or collaboration offers that ask you to click a link or pay for “shipping”.
  • Investment or crypto tips promising returns.
  • Fake sellers who ask you to pay by transfer outside the app.
  • Hacked friends asking for codes or urgent money.

Step 2: Report and block

Open the chat, tap the name, choose Report and pick the scam or fraud option. Then block the account. If you’re reporting a fake business account, also use the profile’s three-dot menu to report impersonation.

Step 3: If you clicked or entered details

Change your Instagram password right away, log out other sessions and check Recent emails in Accounts Center. If you entered a code, treat the account as compromised and follow our hacked-account guide. If you used the same password elsewhere, change those too.

Step 4: If you paid

Contact your bank or payment provider immediately and ask about stopping or disputing the payment. Report the fraud to your local consumer protection authority or police. Instagram can’t refund money sent to a scammer.

Ten-second checks before you respond to any offer

  • Does the username match the real brand’s verified profile, character for character?
  • Did they contact you first about something you never applied for?
  • Does it ask you to leave Instagram for another app or a link?
  • Is there urgency, a deadline or a penalty?
  • Is payment requested by transfer, gift card or crypto?

Two or more “yes” answers is enough reason to stop. Contact the brand through the contact details on its official website, not the ones in the message.

What not to do

  • Don’t pay “recovery fees” to someone who says they can get lost money back. That’s a second scam.
  • Don’t send ID or codes to anyone in a DM.
  • Don’t post the scammer’s personal details publicly.

YOUR QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

How do I know a message is really from Instagram?+

Official emails come from Instagram’s own domains and appear in your Recent emails list in the app. Instagram doesn’t ask for passwords by DM.

Can Instagram get my money back?+

No. Contact your bank and local authorities.

What if a friend sent it?+

Their account may be compromised. Tell them through another channel.
