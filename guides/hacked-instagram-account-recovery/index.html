Signs your account was hacked

  • Your password suddenly stops working.
  • You receive an email from Instagram saying your email address, phone number or password was changed when you did not do it.
  • Posts, stories or messages appear that you did not send.
  • Your username or profile details have changed.
  • Friends tell you that you are sending them suspicious links or crypto offers.

If you can still log in, change your password immediately, then go to Accounts Center > Password and security to review where you are logged in and remove sessions you do not recognise.

Step 1: Check for Instagram’s security email

When an account’s email address is changed, Instagram sends a message to the previous address. That email typically contains a link to reverse the change and secure the account. Search your inbox (and spam) for recent messages from Instagram. If you find one that mentions a change you did not make, use the link it provides.

Only trust emails from Instagram or Meta domains. You can check recent official emails from inside the app under the security settings once you are back in.

Step 2: Use instagram.com/hacked

Instagram’s dedicated recovery flow lives at instagram.com/hacked. It is the official starting point for compromised accounts and works from a browser or from the “Forgot password?” / “Need more help?” options on the login screen.

  1. Open instagram.com/hacked and choose the option that matches your situation – for example, that your account was hacked or that you can no longer access the email or phone on it.
  2. Enter the username, email or phone number associated with the account.
  3. Request a security code to an email or phone you still control.
  4. Follow the prompts. If Instagram cannot send a code to something you control, it may offer identity verification instead.

Step 3: Verify your identity

For accounts with photos of you, Instagram may ask for a video selfie, turning your head in different directions. Instagram uses it to confirm that you are a real person and that you match the account. For accounts without photos of the owner, it may ask other questions about the account, such as the email or phone used to sign up and the type of device used.

Use the device and network you normally use for Instagram where possible, follow the prompts exactly, and complete the process once. Verification requests are reviewed by Instagram; there is no published review time.

Step 4: Secure the email and phone first

Hackers often take over the email account first. If your email was compromised, recover it through your email provider’s own recovery process before going further – otherwise the attacker can simply reset Instagram again. Change that email password, turn on two-factor authentication there, and check its forwarding rules for anything you did not set up.

Step 5: Lock the account down

Instagram recommends these steps if you can still log in or have just regained access:

  • Change your password, or send yourself a password reset email.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication.
  • Confirm that the phone number and email in your settings are yours.
  • Check Accounts Center and remove any linked accounts you don’t recognise.
  • Revoke access for any suspicious third-party apps.

Also look through your posts, messages and any linked Facebook Page or ad account for changes the hacker made, and warn followers if scam messages went out in your name.

Mistakes to avoid after a hack

  • Using “hack it back” offers. Hacking breaks Instagram’s terms and puts your account at more risk. Use the official recovery flow or a service that works from the notice, not your login.
  • Giving anyone your login codes. No genuine helper needs them. A message asking you to forward a code you’ve just received is a classic takeover trick.
  • Opening a new account in a panic. Keep the focus on the original. Meta says it disables accounts made to get around restrictions, which matters if the old account ends up disabled.
  • Using a shared or old email as your secure email. If the hacker can read it, they can undo your recovery.
  • Giving up after one failed selfie. Instagram says you can submit a new video if the first wasn’t accepted.

What to gather before you start

Recovery is easier if you have the facts ready before you open the recovery flow:

  • The username (and any old usernames) of the account.
  • The email addresses and phone numbers you have ever used on it.
  • Roughly when you created the account and what device you used.
  • Screenshots of any security emails from Instagram about changed details.
  • Photos of you that appear on the account, if any – these matter for video selfie checks.

Work from a device and network you have used with Instagram before. Logging in from a familiar phone and home Wi-Fi gives Instagram’s systems a more consistent signal than a brand-new device in a new location.

If the account was disabled after the hack

When a hacked account is later disabled, the disabled notice usually appears once you get back to the login screen. If so:

  1. Recover access first using the steps above where you can, so the notice reaches you and not the hacker.
  2. Screenshot the disabled notice, any deadline and the dates of the security emails you received.
  3. Use the review or appeal option on the notice. If there’s a box to explain, say clearly that the account was compromised, roughly when, and that the activity wasn’t yours.
  4. Complete any identity check Instagram asks for.

If your notice shows a countdown, see our guide to appealing an Instagram suspension, and for disabled accounts generally see Instagram account disabled.

Questions people also ask (2026)

Hacked and then disabled: which comes first?

Start with the hacked route (instagram.com/hacked or facebook.com/hacked), not the normal appeal, so the record shows the account was taken. Secure the linked email and phone first, then appeal the disable if a button appears. Report the scam where you live (table below).

Disabled and no option to send ID?

Meta often asks for a video selfie instead of a document, or shows no upload at all. Record the selfie once, in good light, with no filters or glasses. If no option appears, screenshot the screen with the date. Sending repeated requests does not help.

Stuck on “we’re reviewing your info” after verification?

That screen means the selfie or document was received. Do not resubmit unless asked, and do not create new accounts while waiting, which can be treated as getting around the decision. If nothing changes for weeks, an EU user can look at Appeals Centre Europe once Meta’s own appeal is done.

How long does an appeal take?

Meta does not commit to a timeline. Waits vary widely, and identity checks can add to them. The appeal window shown on a disable notice is usually about 180 days, so note the date on yours.

Login and recovery questions (2026)

What if a hacker changed the email and phone?

Check the old inbox for a security email from Meta about the change. It can include a link to undo it. On Instagram, use “My login details were changed” in the login help, or instagram.com/hacked.

Does a recognised device help?

Often, yes. Meta may trust a phone, browser or network you have used to log in before, so try recovery from that device and connection rather than a new one.
