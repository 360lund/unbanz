Meta “unacceptable business practices” ad restriction: what it means now and what to do

“Unacceptable business practices” is Meta's label for ads it believes are deceptive or scam-like. It is one of the hardest restrictions to overturn, because it is about trust in the advertiser, not one image or word. Here is what it covers, how Meta changed it in 2026, and what a legitimate business can do.

What the policy covers

Meta's Unacceptable Business Practices policy says ads must not promote products, services, schemes or offers using deceptive or misleading practices, including those meant to scam people out of money or personal information. Its guidelines say ads can't:

  • use deceptive or exaggerated claims about the success of a product or service;
  • use deceptive or exaggerated claims about health-related benefits;
  • use the image of a famous person with misleading tactics to bait people into engaging;
  • promise financial benefits by misrepresenting an entity, industry association or news outlet.

Meta says it often sees these violations in investment or banking opportunities, health or weight loss, “free” product schemes, and products with functionality that doesn't exist. It also says that when it detects potentially suspicious behaviour, or ads in categories likely to be targeted by scammers, it may require advertisers to complete additional verification.

What changed in 2026: Prohibited Commercial Practices

In a September 2026 update, Meta said it is retiring the standalone Unacceptable Business Practices ad policy and consolidating those protections into a Community Standard called Prohibited Commercial Practices, which applies to both ads and organic content.

Meta says violations can lead to a range of actions depending on severity: removing content and ads, reducing distribution, restricting advertising accounts, limiting features, requiring advertiser verification and disabling accounts. It also says penalties can be extended beyond a single ad account to associated users, Pages and business portfolios with repeat violations. In practice, an “unacceptable business practices” notice and a “prohibited commercial practices” notice now point to the same family of rules.

What legitimate advertisers get caught on

  • Before-and-after or “guaranteed results” claims in health, beauty, fitness and coaching.
  • Income claims such as “make £5,000 a month” for courses, trading, dropshipping or affiliate offers.
  • Celebrity or media logos (“as seen on…”) without permission.
  • Fake urgency and fake discounts, such as countdown timers that reset or prices that were never charged.
  • Landing pages that don't match the ad, or lack basic business information such as contact details, refund policy and company name.
  • New advertisers in high-risk categories who haven't completed verification.

How to request a review

  1. In Business Support Home, open Account status overview and find what is restricted: the ad, ad account, Page, person or portfolio.
  2. Fix what you can before requesting a review: remove exaggerated claims, add clear business information to the landing page, and remove borrowed celebrity or media references.
  3. Complete any advertiser or business verification Meta asks for, with documents matching your legal business name.
  4. As an admin, select the restricted item and choose Request review under What you can do, as described on Meta's review help page.
  5. For individual rejected ads, request another review in Business Support Home or edit the ad. Meta's rejected ad page says it aims to decide within 48 hours.

Make your business easy to trust

Meta's business help page on this policy recommends creating a reputable presence on Facebook, Instagram and your landing page, for example by giving customers a way to contact you, and using your organic presence to show who you are if you are new. Reviewers are asking “is this a real business I'd be comfortable sending people to?”

What not to do

  • Don't open new ad accounts or portfolios to keep running the same offer. Meta says repeat violations can extend penalties across associated users, Pages and portfolios.
  • Don't rephrase the same claims with softer words and resubmit. The issue is the claim, not the wording.
  • Don't use “ad account rental” services. They are a fast route to wider restrictions.

An honest note

If the offer genuinely relies on misleading claims or impersonating trusted brands, no appeal will fix that, and we won't take it on. If you are a real business that over-claimed, a cleaned-up funnel and a clear, honest review request is the realistic path.

YOUR QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

What is unacceptable business practices on Facebook ads?+

It is Meta's policy against ads that promote products, services or offers using deceptive or misleading practices, including scams meant to take people's money or personal information.

Is unacceptable business practices still a Meta policy?+

Meta said in September 2026 that it is retiring the standalone ad policy and consolidating it into the Prohibited Commercial Practices Community Standard, which covers both ads and organic content.

Can an unacceptable business practices restriction be reversed?+

Sometimes, especially when the business is genuine and the issue was over-claiming or missing information. Fix the ads and landing pages, complete any verification, and request a review as an admin.

Why was I asked to verify my business?+

Meta says it may require additional verification when it detects potentially suspicious behaviour or when advertisers run ads in categories often targeted by scammers.
