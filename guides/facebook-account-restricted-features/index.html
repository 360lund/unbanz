Facebook account restricted from features: what it means and how to fix it

You can still log in to Facebook, but you can't post in groups, comment, send messages or go Live. That is a feature restriction, and it is different from a locked or disabled account. Here is how Meta's restriction system works and what you can do about it.

What a feature restriction is

A feature restriction limits what you can do on Facebook for a period of time, while the account itself stays active. Facebook's help pages say that for most violations you'll first receive a warning, and if you go on to violate again you may lose access to some features. They also say that if multiple pieces of content are removed within a short period, Facebook will only place a short-term restriction on your account.

This is not the same as:

  • a locked account, which is a security hold you clear by confirming your identity (see Facebook account locked);
  • a suspended or disabled account, which you can't use at all (see Facebook account disabled appeal).

How long restrictions last

Meta publishes the restriction ladder for most violations on Facebook on its Restricting accounts page:

  • One strike: a warning.
  • Two to six strikes: restrictions from specific features, like posting in groups, for a limited time.
  • Seven strikes: a one-day restriction from creating content, including posting, commenting and creating a Page.
  • Eight strikes: a 3-day restriction from creating content.
  • Nine strikes: a 7-day restriction.
  • Ten or more strikes: a 30-day restriction.

For more severe policies, Meta says you may get longer restrictions on top, such as being blocked from creating ads or using Facebook Live, starting from the first strike. Meta's Counting strikes page says strikes expire after one year.

Check your Account Status

Facebook's Account Status page explains how to see what is going on:

  1. Click your profile picture in the top right of Facebook.
  2. Select Help & support, then Account Status.

Account Status shows the status of your main profile, the status of Pages and groups you manage, and any restrictions, which can include Community Standards strikes, restrictions, limited monetization and your recommendations status. Screenshot it with the date visible.

How to appeal a restriction

  1. In Account Status, open the removed content that led to the restriction.
  2. If you believe the removal was a mistake, choose the option to request a review and follow the steps.
  3. Keep your explanation short and factual: what the post was and why it follows the rules.

Meta says that if it finds your content did follow the Community Standards, it will restore the content and remove the strike and restriction so they won't count against you later.

If you agree the content broke the rules, there is no useful appeal. Wait for the end date shown and avoid repeating the violation, because new strikes extend the ladder.

Restrictions that aren't about strikes

Not every limit comes from a content violation. Facebook also limits activity that looks like spam, such as sending many friend requests or messages quickly, or posting the same link in many groups. Those limits usually clear on their own once the activity stops. Marketplace has its own rules too; see our guide to Facebook Marketplace bans.

Pages and groups

If you manage a Page or group, violations can affect it as well. Meta says Pages and groups that repeatedly violate policies may be removed from recommendations and have their distribution reduced. Check Page Quality or Group Quality, and see our guide to disabled Facebook groups.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Making a second profile to keep posting. Facebook allows one personal account per person, and Meta's policies cover accounts used to evade restrictions.
  • Reposting removed content in a different form. It can count again.
  • Ignoring warnings. The first strike is a warning precisely so you can adjust before restrictions begin.
  • Paying anyone to “remove restrictions.” Only Meta's review can do that.

Staying off the ladder

  • Read the Community Standards sections that apply to what you usually post.
  • Avoid sharing graphic, sexual or hateful material even to criticise it, unless the context is clear.
  • Pace friend requests, group posts and messages.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication so no one else can post as you.

YOUR QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

How long is a Facebook restriction?+

For most violations, Meta's ladder ranges from a warning at one strike to a 30-day restriction from creating content at ten or more strikes. Account Status shows your specific restrictions and how long they last.

Do Facebook strikes expire?+

Yes. Meta says all strikes on Facebook or Instagram expire after one year.

Can I still use Messenger while restricted?+

It depends on the restriction. Some restrictions cover messaging, others only posting or groups. Account Status lists exactly what is limited.

Will appealing make my restriction longer?+

No. Requesting a review does not add strikes. If the content is found to follow the rules, Meta restores it and removes the strike and restriction.
