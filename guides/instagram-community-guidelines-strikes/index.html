Instagram Community Guidelines strikes explained: how they add up and expire

Short answer: a strike is added when Meta removes content for breaking its rules, and strikes add up to restrictions. Check your strikes in Account Status and disagree with any removal that is a mistake.

Every time Meta removes a post for breaking its rules, it may add a strike to your account. Strikes are what turn a single removal into restrictions and, eventually, a disabled account. Here is how the system works, using Meta's own published rules. Clients share video reviews of working with us.

What a strike is

Meta's Counting strikes page says that if you post content that goes against the Community Standards, it will be removed and Meta “may then apply a strike” to your Facebook or Instagram account. Whether a strike is applied depends on the severity of the content, the context it was shared in, and when it was posted.

A strike is not the same as a removal. Some removals do not add a strike, and several removals can be counted as one.

The rules Meta publishes

From Meta's Transparency Center:

  • Old content is not counted for most violations. Meta says it won't count strikes on violating content posted over 90 days ago for most violations, or over 4 years ago for more severe violations.
  • Some removals carry no strike. For example, when someone shares their own financial information and it is removed to prevent fraud, or when Meta has extra context about the violation.
  • Batches can count once. If Meta removes multiple pieces of content at once without notifying you at each removal, it may count them as a single strike.
  • Strikes expire. Meta says all strikes on Facebook or Instagram expire after one year.
  • Severe violations skip the ladder. Some violations, such as child sexual exploitation content, can get an account disabled after one occurrence.

What happens as strikes add up

Meta's Restricting accounts page says that for most violations your first strike results in a warning with no further restrictions, and later strikes can mean losing access to some features for longer periods. Meta publishes a detailed ladder for Facebook: a warning at one strike, feature restrictions at two to six, then content-creation restrictions of one day, three days, seven days and 30 days at seven, eight, nine and ten or more strikes.

Meta notes that while it counts strikes on both Facebook and Instagram, those specific restrictions apply to Facebook accounts. On Instagram, the effects you are more likely to see are content removals, features you can't use, reduced recommendation eligibility and, eventually, the account being disabled. Meta's Disabling accounts page says that after five strikes you may receive additional 30-day restrictions from creating content, or the account may be removed, depending on severity and frequency.

How to see your strikes

  1. Open Instagram and go to your profile.
  2. Open the menu, then tap Account Status below “More info and support”.
  3. Review Removed content and Features you can't use.

Instagram's Account Status page says you can see content removed for going against the Community Standards that may lead to your account being taken down, and features you can no longer use. Threads has its own Account Status, which notes that you usually'll get a warning before losing access to your account.

How to get a strike removed

The only official way is a successful review. Meta says that if it finds your content did follow the Community Standards, it will put the content back and remove the strike and restriction so they won't count against you in the future.

  1. In Account Status, open the removed item.
  2. If a review is offered, request it once and explain briefly why the content follows the rules (for example, it was satire, news reporting or educational context).
  3. Wait for the outcome in Account Status or your notifications.

If you agree the content broke the rules, the honest strategy is to let the strike expire and avoid repeating the violation.

Managing risk if you have several strikes

  • Slow down on borderline content until the oldest strikes expire.
  • Audit your recent posts. Remove anything you would not want reviewed, before someone reports it.
  • Watch shared logins. A team member or agency posting on your behalf can earn strikes for you.
  • Keep a log of removal dates so you know when each strike reaches its one-year expiry.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Starting a backup account to keep posting. Meta's Account Integrity policy covers accounts created to evade enforcement.
  • Requesting reviews on everything. Use reviews for genuine mistakes; they are not a way to wipe a record.
  • Paying anyone who claims they can “clear strikes.” Only Meta's review process can remove a strike.

YOUR QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

How long do Instagram strikes last?+

Meta says all strikes on Facebook or Instagram expire after one year.

How many strikes before Instagram disables an account?+

There is no single number for every account. Meta says severe violations can lead to an account being disabled after one occurrence, and that after five strikes accounts may face 30-day restrictions or removal, depending on severity and frequency.

Does every removed post count as a strike?+

No. Meta says some removals do not carry a strike, old content is not counted for most violations, and several removals made at once may count as one strike.

Can a strike be removed?+

Yes, if a review finds the content did follow the rules. Meta says it then restores the content and removes the strike and related restriction.
