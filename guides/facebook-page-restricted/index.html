Facebook Page restricted or unpublished: how to fix it

When a business Page disappears from public view or loses features, customers notice fast. The fix depends on one question: did someone with access unpublish the Page, or did Meta restrict it? Here is how to tell, and what to do about each.

Unpublished by an admin, or restricted by Meta?

“Unpublished” can mean two quite different things.

  • Unpublished by someone with access. Facebook lets people with Facebook access to a Page unpublish it themselves. According to Facebook’s help page Unpublish and republish a Page, an unpublished Page isn’t visible or searchable to anyone without access, can’t interact with other profiles or Pages, can’t start new ad campaigns, and existing campaigns are paused. Admins can still switch into it and edit it. Unpublishing a parent Page also unpublishes its child Pages, such as store location Pages.
  • Taken down or limited by Meta. Facebook’s page Why Facebook Pages get taken down or have limits placed on them explains that Pages that don’t follow the Community Standards may be taken down or have limits placed on them. Then there is normally a notice on the Page and an appeal option.

If nobody on your team remembers unpublishing it and there’s no notice, check who has access. An unexpected admin can be a sign the Page was hacked.

If an admin unpublished it: republish the Page

Facebook’s steps to publish a Page again are:

  1. Switch to the Page.
  2. Click your Page profile picture in the top right, then click your Page.
  3. Under Manage Page, click Settings.
  4. On the left under Your information, click Access and control.
  5. Click Publish Page, then Continue, then Confirm.

Child Pages that were unpublished separately need republishing separately, and they can’t be republished until the parent Page is. Paused ad campaigns won’t restart on their own, so check Ads Manager afterwards.

If Meta restricted it: check Page Status

Facebook’s Page Status tool shows recent Community Standards violations, fact-checked content, intellectual property violations, clickbait, sharing too much content from other sources, event takedowns and content that isn’t eligible to be suggested. It also shows violations caused by other people who manage the Page, and who posted the content. To open it:

  1. Log in to Facebook and click your profile photo in the top right.
  2. Click See all profiles and select the Page.
  3. Click the Page profile picture in the top right.
  4. Click Settings & privacy, then Settings.
  5. In the left menu click Page setup, then Page Status.

Under Recent Violations, click See Details on each item. For Community Standards violations you can either accept the decision or request a review, depending on the violation. Facebook notes that Page Status doesn’t show every kind of violation yet, such as some breaches of its Page Policies.

How to appeal a restricted or unpublished Page

Facebook’s instruction is direct: if you believe your Page was taken down or received limits by mistake, go to the Page and click Appeal at the top. Before you do:

  • Screenshot the notice, Page Status and the dates.
  • Read the specific violations and check whether any posts genuinely break the rules. Fix what you can first.
  • In any explanation, refer to the policy Meta named and explain plainly why the Page doesn’t break it. Keep it short and factual.

If the issue is advertising rather than the Page itself, for example the Page is restricted from advertising, the review happens in Meta Business Support Home. See our guide to a restricted Meta ad account. Facebook also says that a Page which severely or repeatedly breaks the Ads Policies can be taken down along with the ad account, and that this decision can be appealed through Ads Manager or the policy-disabled ad account form linked from its help page.

Common reasons Pages get restricted

Facebook lists these common reasons:

  • not all admins have authentic profiles;
  • people who manage the Page have acted against the Community Standards;
  • a misleading Page name, or a big name change that could mislead people;
  • content that misleads followers, or artificially boosting popularity;
  • hateful conduct;
  • severe or repeated breaches of the Ads Policies;
  • no admin with full control, and then the Page is automatically deactivated and scheduled for deletion. Facebook says you can submit a Page admin dispute if the last person with full control is no longer active.

Because admins’ own profiles count, a disabled personal account can drag a Page down with it. Our guide to a disabled Facebook account covers that side.

If the Page was hacked

If someone hacked or scammed you or another admin and removed your access, Facebook recommends its Page recovery form. You need to be logged in, and the form only lets you choose a Page you recently lost access to. Facebook says it aims to reply within a day, though some reviews take longer, and it can only act if it can confirm the Page was compromised.

Mistakes that keep a Page offline

  • Making a duplicate Page. It starts from zero and can be treated as getting around enforcement.
  • Deleting everything in a panic. Remove what genuinely breaks the rules, but keep your evidence and screenshots.
  • Leaving unknown admins in place. Check Page access under Page setup and remove anyone you don’t recognise.
  • Having only one person with full control. If that profile is disabled, the Page can be deactivated.
  • Handing your login to a “Page recovery agent”. No genuine helper needs your password or codes.

YOUR QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

Why was my Facebook Page unpublished?+

Either someone with access unpublished it in Settings, or Meta took it down or limited it for not following the Community Standards. Check for a notice on the Page and look at Page Status.

How do I appeal a restricted Facebook Page?+

Facebook says to go to the Page and click Appeal at the top. For individual violations, open Page Status, click See Details and request a review where offered.

Can I see why my Facebook Page was restricted?+

Usually. Page Status lists recent violations and who posted the content, though Facebook says it doesn’t yet show every type of violation.

What happens to my ads when a Page is unpublished?+

Facebook says an unpublished Page can’t start new ad campaigns and existing campaigns are paused. Turn them back on in Ads Manager once the Page is published again.

My Page was hacked and I lost admin access. What now?+

Log in and use Facebook’s Page recovery form for hacked Pages. It aims to reply within a day, but some reviews take longer.
