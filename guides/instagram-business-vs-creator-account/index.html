Instagram Business vs Creator account: which should you choose?

Short answer: Business suits brands that sell or run ads. Creator suits personal brands and influencers. You can switch any time from Account type and tools.

The choice affects tools, music and how messages are sorted, and it matters more if you already run ads. It’s reversible, but a bad choice can disrupt linked Pages and ad setups.

What to do first

Write what you actually need from the account: sell products, take bookings, run ads, grow a following, use music freely. That list picks the account for you. Check whether your profile is private, because switching to a professional account makes it public.

Step 1: Compare what each is for

  • Business: contact buttons, action buttons, shop and booking tools, ad access and insights. Built for companies and services.
  • Creator: insights, profile categories, messaging tools to manage heavy inboxes and tools for monetisation. Built for people building an audience.

Step 2: Check music and content rules

Music licensing differs. Business accounts typically have a more limited music library than creator accounts, because commercial use needs different rights. If you’re a business using trending audio, you may see restrictions. Check what’s available to you before planning content.

Step 3: Switch

Go to Profile, then Menu, then Account type and tools and Switch account type. Pick Business, Creator or Personal. Switching to professional makes a private account public and accepts pending follow requests, so review your followers first.

Step 4: Think about ads and linked Pages

Running ads from Instagram usually involves a Facebook Page and an ad account. Changing the account type can change what tools you see, so check ad access afterward. If your business uses a Meta Business portfolio, make sure the right people still have access.

Quick decision checklist

  • You sell products or services and want buttons for calls, directions or bookings: Business.
  • You plan to run ads regularly: Business, and set up a Page and ad account properly.
  • You are a person-led brand with a big inbox and collaborations: Creator.
  • You want the widest music choice for Reels: Creator may fit better.
  • You want a private account: Personal. Professional accounts are public.

Mixed accounts: a person who is also a brand

Many small brands are one person. If you post as yourself and sell sometimes, pick the type that matches your main use and put the other details in the bio. If you later need to run proper ads, switch and set up your Page and ad account then. Switching doesn’t remove your posts or followers.

What changes when you switch, and how to switch safely

Switching account types is reversible, but a few things shift at the moment of change. Switching a private account to a professional one makes it public. Insights start collecting from the switch, not before. Features tied to a type, such as shopping or certain contact buttons, appear or disappear. If you rely on scheduled content or third-party tools, check that they still connect afterwards.

Before switching, note your current settings and take a screenshot of your profile and contact options so you can restore them if needed.

Access, roles and security

If several people work on the account, a Meta Business portfolio gives clearer roles than sharing a password. Whichever type you use, turn on two-factor authentication and keep a record of who has access.

What not to do

  • Don’t switch in the middle of a campaign without testing.
  • Don’t keep pretending to be a business when you’re a person, or the reverse. Choose honestly.
  • Don’t forget to back up insights before you change.

YOUR QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

Can I switch back to personal?+

Yes. You can change account type any time. You lose professional tools like insights and ad tools.

Do I need a Facebook Page for a business account?+

Not for every feature, but ads and some shopping tools work best with one linked.

Which is better for reach?+

Neither account type is promised more reach. Content quality and eligibility drive recommendations.
