What the policy says

Meta's Circumventing Systems policy says ads must not use tactics intended to circumvent the ad review process, including techniques that attempt to disguise the ad's content or destination (landing) page. Meta explains that when an advertiser tries to get around ad review, “it erodes trust in the advertiser and gives the impression that they are acting with bad intent,” and that every part of an ad must follow the rules and be available for review, including text, images, video, thumbnails and destination pages.

The examples Meta lists include ads that:

  • limit Meta's access to evaluate the destination page, such as through cloaking;
  • use unicode characters or symbols in ad text with the intent to obfuscate words or phrases;
  • obscure images to get around the review process.

Its close relative: evading enforcement

Meta's Advertising Standards also say advertisers must not evade or attempt to evade review and enforcement actions. A frequent example is creating new business assets after earlier ones were restricted. Meta's Account Integrity policy likewise covers business assets created or repurposed to evade a previous removal, or owned by the same person or entity as a disabled account.

Setups that can look like circumvention

In what we see, legitimate advertisers get flagged because of:

  • Redirect chains from tracking or link-shortening tools, so the reviewer and the user land in different places;
  • Geo- or device-based redirects that show Meta's crawler a different page from real visitors;
  • Landing pages that block bots, firewalls or “are you human” screens that stop Meta's crawler from seeing the page;
  • Text styled with special characters, such as “F R E E” or letters swapped for symbols, to make ads stand out;
  • Re-uploading a rejected ad with tiny changes, again and again;
  • New ad accounts or Pages opened right after an older one was restricted.

Step 1: Fix the setup before you appeal

  1. Open the landing page from the ad on a clean phone and a desktop in another country if you can. Confirm everyone sees the same page.
  2. Remove unnecessary redirects. Point ads straight at the final URL and keep tracking in URL parameters.
  3. Whitelist Meta's crawler in your firewall or bot protection, and remove interstitials and age gates you don't legally need.
  4. Rewrite ad text in plain language. No symbol substitutions, spaced-out words or hidden text.
  5. Make sure the ad matches the page. The product, price and claims in the ad should appear on the landing page.
  6. Stop creating new assets to replace restricted ones.

Step 2: Request a review

Meta's request a review page explains that if you believe your business portfolio, ad account, Page or user account was incorrectly restricted from advertising, an admin can request a review in Business Support Home under Account status overview › the restricted account › What you can do › Request review.

For a single rejected ad, Meta's rejected ad page says you can request another review in Business Support Home and that it aims to decide within 48 hours.

What to say

Be specific and factual: what the business sells, which domain the ads point to, what you have changed (for example, “removed the redirect from our link tool”, “allowed Meta's crawler through our firewall”, “rewrote ad text without special characters”), and that the landing page is now the same for every visitor. If you genuinely did not intend to hide anything, say so plainly.

An honest note on cloaking

If an account was intentionally showing reviewers a different page from users, there is no wording that makes that acceptable, and no outside service can make it go away. The only sustainable path is to stop, fix the funnel and accept Meta's decision on the assets involved.

What not to do

  • Don't buy “aged” or “agency” ad accounts to keep running. That is the evading-enforcement pattern Meta describes.
  • Don't use “anti-ban” cloaking tools or “white page” setups.
  • Don't submit several appeals for the same asset at once.

Ad account questions people also ask (2026)

Where do I request the review?

Go to Account Quality in Meta Business Suite (business.facebook.com/accountquality), select the ad account, and use Request review if it is shown. For a personal ad account, open Accounts Center, then Account status.

How long does an ad account review take?

Meta does not state one. While you wait, do not open new ad accounts or move spend to a fresh profile, which can be read as circumventing systems. Check Account Quality for status rather than resubmitting.

Business verification or ID check pending: what helps?

Use documents that match the business portfolio name and address exactly, such as an EIN letter (US), Companies House record (UK), CRA business number (Canada), KvK extract (Netherlands) or Kbis/SIREN (France). A name mismatch is one of the most common reasons verification fails.

YOUR QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

What does circumventing systems mean on Facebook ads?+

It means Meta believes an ad or advertiser used tactics to get around ad review, such as cloaking the landing page, obfuscating words with symbols or obscuring images.

Can I appeal a circumventing systems restriction?+

If a review option is offered, yes. An admin can request it in Business Support Home. Fix the technical cause first so the review finds a clean setup.

Can a redirect cause a circumventing systems flag?+

Redirects that send Meta's reviewers and real users to different pages can look like cloaking. Point ads directly at the final landing page where possible.

Should I open a new ad account after a circumventing systems ban?+

No. Meta's policies treat new assets created to evade a previous restriction as a violation, which can make the situation worse.
