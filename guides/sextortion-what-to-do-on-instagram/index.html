Sextortion on Instagram: what to do right now

Short answer: don’t pay, stop replying, block and report the account, keep evidence, and use StopNCII.org (18+) or NCMEC’s Take It Down (under 18) to help stop images spreading.

Sextortion is a crime, and it’s not your fault. Someone threatens to share intimate images unless you pay or send more. Acting calmly and quickly protects you, and there are free tools designed for exactly this.

What to do first

Stop replying. Don’t pay and don’t send more images. Paying rarely ends it, and threats often continue. If you’re under 18 or feel in danger, tell a trusted adult or contact local police straight away.

Step 1: Keep evidence

Screenshot the messages, the profile and any payment demands before you block. Save usernames, links and dates. Don’t delete the chat yet. This is useful for the police and for Instagram.

Step 2: Block and report

Report the chat from the conversation menu, choosing the option that fits what they’re doing, then block the account. Instagram’s safety pages explain how to report intimate image threats.

Step 3: Use image-removal tools

StopNCII.org lets adults create a digital fingerprint of an image on their own device so participating platforms can detect and block it. The image isn’t uploaded. For images taken when you were under 18, NCMEC’s Take It Down service works in a similar way. Both are free and run by recognised organisations.

Step 4: Contact the authorities and get support

Report to your local police or your country’s online-crime reporting service. Talk with someone you trust, or a support organisation in your country. Shame is what blackmailers rely on, and you’re not alone.

Step 5: Tighten your account

Set the account to private, review followers, turn on Hidden Words and limit who can message you. Change your password and enable two-factor authentication.

How these threats usually unfold

Sextortion often starts with a friendly new account, sometimes using attractive photos, that quickly moves the chat to a more private setting, shares an intimate image first and asks for one in return. The moment an image is sent, the tone changes to threats: pay, send more or the image goes to your followers and family. Time pressure is part of the technique. Knowing the pattern helps you step out of it, because the threats are designed to make you panic rather than think.

If you’re a parent or carer

Stay calm and make it clear the young person isn’t in trouble. Help them stop contact, save evidence and report. For under-18s, NCMEC’s Take It Down service and the police are the main routes. Blame shuts conversations down, and the first reaction you give often decides whether they tell you the whole story.

What not to do

  • Don’t pay. It often leads to more demands.
  • Don’t delete everything in panic. Keep evidence first.
  • Don’t blame yourself. The person making threats is responsible.

YOUR QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

Will paying make it stop?+

Usually not. Many victims report that demands continue.

Does StopNCII upload my image?+

No. It creates a hash on your device, and the image isn’t sent.

Can Instagram remove the account?+

You can report it. Meta doesn’t publish a timeline, so also contact the police.
