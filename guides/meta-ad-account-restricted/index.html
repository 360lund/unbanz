Meta ad account restricted: how to fix it

An ad restriction can stop a business overnight. Most fixes start in the same place – Account Quality – and depend on finding which asset Meta actually restricted. Clients share video reviews of working with us.

Work out what is restricted

Meta’s About advertising restrictions page explains that restrictions can be placed on four different things, and each behaves differently:

AssetWhat a restriction means
Business portfolioThe portfolio isn’t allowed to advertise.
Ad accountThe ad account, its ads and some of its advertising assets are disabled.
PageThe Page isn’t allowed to advertise.
Personal profileThat person can’t advertise or create new ads. Ad accounts where they’re the only user may also be disabled, though other people on the business portfolio, ad account or Page may still be able to advertise.

To see which one you’re dealing with, open Meta Business Support Home on a computer and look at Account status overview. Screenshot each restricted item, the reason shown and the date.

Check Account Quality

Go to Account Quality (business.facebook.com/accountquality). It shows the assets you have access to, any restrictions, the reason Meta gives, and – where available – a Request review button. Select the restricted asset and open the details before you do anything else. Note the policy named; it tells you what the review will be judged against.

Common reasons for restrictions

Meta lists several situations where it may apply advertising restrictions:

  • the advertiser doesn’t follow the Community Standards, Advertising Standards, Commerce Policies or other terms;
  • Meta suspects the business portfolio or its assets have been compromised or hacked;
  • the advertiser doesn’t meet Meta’s two-factor authentication requirements;
  • Meta notices unusual payment or account activity, or high rates of failed or disputed payments.

Restrictions aren’t always a full ban. Meta says they can also mean a daily spending limit, a lower payment threshold, losing some payment or advertising features, or not being able to add admins or create new ad accounts while it investigates unusual activity.

How to request a review

Meta’s Troubleshoot a disabled or restricted account page sets out the route:

  1. On a computer, go to Meta Business Support Home.
  2. From Account Overview, click the restricted account.
  3. Find the What you can do section and follow the steps it shows. Meta says it may ask you to confirm your identity, complete verification, secure your account or request a review.

Fix the cause before you appeal

If the restriction is about security, secure the account first: turn on two-factor authentication for everyone with access and remove people or partners you don’t recognise. If it’s about payments, sort out the failed payment through Meta’s billing tools. If it’s about a policy, read the ads that were rejected and the landing pages they point to. Appealing without addressing an obvious cause is the quickest way to use up a review.

Request a review

Meta’s Request a review for a restricted advertising account page gives these steps:

  1. In Business Support Home, select Account status overview in the left menu.
  2. Select the restricted account.
  3. In the What you can do section, select Request review.
  4. Complete the on-screen steps.

You must be an admin on the account to request a review. Meta says the review is typically completed in 48 hours, although it can sometimes take longer.

Fix the root cause first

Before requesting a review, remove or edit ads that may break the Advertising Standards, check your landing pages match what the ads promise, settle any unpaid balance, remove admins you do not recognise, and turn on two-factor authentication for every person with access. If the account was compromised, secure the profiles first – otherwise the problem can repeat.

Business verification

For many businesses, completing business verification in Business Settings helps establish who is behind the account. It typically involves the legal business name, address, phone number and documents such as a business registration or utility bill. Make sure the details match exactly. Verification won’t stop restrictions on its own, but mismatched details can slow reviews.

Rules worth knowing before you click

  • Reviews are limited. Meta says there is a limited number of times you can request an advertising restriction review, and once the review is completed the decision is final. Make each request count.
  • Asking for a review doesn’t count against you. Meta states that requesting a review won’t hurt your standing.
  • Ads don’t restart by themselves. If the restriction is lifted, ads that were running before won’t restart automatically. Turn them back on in Ads Manager.
  • The 6-month limit. If an ad account is disabled for a policy violation and stays ineligible for reinstatement for 6 months, Meta says any unused prepaid funds may be forfeited where the law allows, and after that the account can’t be reinstated. Meta also reserves the right to disable an account permanently before then in some circumstances.

Mistakes that keep ad accounts blocked

  • Opening new ad accounts or portfolios to get round it. This is treated as circumventing enforcement and can spread restrictions to your other assets and the people on them.
  • Relaunching the same ad. If the creative or landing page was the problem, it will be flagged again.
  • Running a vague appeal. Be specific about the asset, the reason Meta gave and what you have changed.
  • Giving strangers admin access. Adding an unknown “agency” as admin or sharing your login can lead to a hack and fresh restrictions. Never share access tokens or card details either.
  • Ignoring the people on the account. A restriction on one admin’s personal profile can be the real blocker. Check every user, not just the ad account.

Avoiding the next restriction

  • Warm up new ad accounts gradually instead of spending heavily on day one.
  • Keep at least two trusted admins with two-factor authentication.
  • Use one payment method per ad account where possible and keep it in good standing.
  • Review ads against the Advertising Standards before launch, especially in sensitive categories such as finance, health and employment.
  • Do not create new accounts to get around restrictions.

What to prepare for a review

Reviews go more smoothly when the facts are clear and consistent across everything Meta can see:

  • Screenshots of the restriction in Account Quality, including the policy named and the date.
  • A list of the ads that were rejected, with what you have changed since.
  • Your website, refund policy and contact details – landing pages should clearly show who you are.
  • Business registration and address details that match the information in Business Settings.
  • Payment method details and confirmation that any outstanding balance is cleared.
  • A note of which people have admin access and when they were added.

Keep your explanation short. Describe what your business does, what you advertise and what you fixed. Avoid copying generic “appeal templates” from the internet; reviewers see the same wording often and it says nothing about your account.

YOUR QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

Where do I appeal a restricted ad account?+

In Account Quality at business.facebook.com/accountquality. Select the asset and choose Request review.

How long does an ad account review take?+

Usually a few days, sometimes longer. Submit one complete request.

Can I just open a new ad account?+

No. Meta may restrict accounts made to get around restrictions, so the new one can be restricted too.

How many times can I request a review of an ad account restriction?+

Meta says there is a limited number of review requests and that once a review is completed the decision is final. It doesn’t publish an exact number.

Will my ads start again after the restriction is lifted?+

No. Meta says ads that were running before the restriction aren’t restarted automatically. Turn them back on in Ads Manager.

What happens to my prepaid ad balance if the account stays disabled?+

Meta says that if an ad account disabled for a policy violation stays ineligible for reinstatement for 6 months, unused prepaid funds may be forfeited where allowed by law, and the account can’t be reinstated after that.

Who can request a review for a restricted ad account?+

Meta says you must be an admin on the account to request a review.
