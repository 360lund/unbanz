Instagram asked for your ID: what’s accepted and how to submit it safely

Short answer: upload only inside the Instagram screen that asked for it. Instagram accepts one government photo ID or two other documents, and your name and photo or date of birth must match.

An ID request can feel alarming, especially when you’re already locked out. It is a normal part of confirming identity or age. It’s also a favourite hook for scammers, so the first question is whether the request is real.

What to do first

Make sure the request appears inside the Instagram app or on instagram.com when you log in or follow an official recovery link. If it arrived by DM, text or an email you can’t match to the app’s list of recent emails, don’t upload anything.

Step 1: Read why you’re being asked

Instagram may ask for ID to confirm your identity after a lock, to check your age, or as part of an appeal. The screen tells you which. The reason matters because it tells you whether a video selfie, a photo ID or both are expected.

Step 2: Pick an accepted document

Instagram’s help page lists the accepted types. In general you can send one government-issued photo ID, or if you don’t have one, two non-government documents. At least one should show your photo and age or date of birth, and the name should match the account. Check the live list on the help page, because acceptable documents vary by country.

Step 3: Take a clear photo

  • Use good light and a plain surface.
  • Keep all four corners of the document in frame.
  • Avoid glare and shadows over text and the photo.
  • Don’t edit or crop the image beyond what the screen asks for.

Step 4: Submit once and wait

Upload through the official screen, then wait for Instagram’s response by email and in the app. Meta doesn’t publish a timeline. Instagram says it stores IDs securely, keeps them private from other users, and deletes the copy within a set period after the check. Read the current wording on the help page so you know what applies in your region.

If the name on your ID doesn’t match the account

This is common: a stage name, a business name or a married name. Instagram’s help page asks for documents where the name matches the account, but you can send documents that show the name you use. Two non-government documents, such as a bank statement and a utility bill, can together show the name and your photo or date of birth. Choose ones that are current and clearly readable, and follow the on-screen wording about what is acceptable in your country.

If the account name is a brand and the ID is your personal name, explain the link in any free-text box the form provides. Keep it factual and short.

What not to do

  • Don’t send your ID to a person who says they work for Instagram, in DMs or by email.
  • Don’t use someone else’s ID or a fabricated one. It’s against the rules and can end the appeal.
  • Don’t resubmit many times if the first upload is still processing.

What happens after you submit your ID

Once the upload goes through, Instagram usually shows a confirmation screen or sends an email. From that point the decision is with Meta, and there is no public timeline. A few things are worth knowing while you wait:

  • Keep the confirmation. Screenshot the confirmation screen and save any email. It shows when you submitted and which account it was for.
  • Watch your email, including spam. Replies from Instagram can ask for a second document or a different photo.
  • Don’t create a new account to get around the request. It can be linked to the original and make things harder.
  • If the account later shows as suspended or disabled, follow the options on that screen. Our suspended account appeal guide covers the steps.

ID request or video selfie: which one did you get?

Instagram uses two different checks and people often mix them up.

  • ID request: you are asked to upload a photo of an official document, sometimes with a code written next to it. It is usually about proving who owns the account or confirming your age.
  • Video selfie: you are asked to turn your head in front of the camera. It is usually about confirming that a real person is behind the account. See our video selfie verification guide.

Read the exact wording on your screen. If it mentions age, our page on suspended for age verification explains that route.

If the upload fails or is rejected

Try again once with a clearer photo, on the same device, with the latest app. If the account name differs from your ID, explain this in any text box on the form. Keep in mind that a rejection can often be followed by a new submission if the screen offers one.

Questions people also ask (2026)

Stuck on “we’re reviewing your info” after verification?

That screen means the selfie or document was received. Do not resubmit unless asked, and do not create new accounts while waiting, which can be treated as getting around the decision. If nothing changes for weeks, an EU user can look at Appeals Centre Europe once Meta’s own appeal is done.

Disabled and no option to send ID?

Meta often asks for a video selfie instead of a document, or shows no upload at all. Record the selfie once, in good light, with no filters or glasses. If no option appears, screenshot the screen with the date. Sending repeated requests does not help.

How long does an appeal take?

Meta does not commit to a timeline. Waits vary widely, and identity checks can add to them. The appeal window shown on a disable notice is usually about 180 days, so note the date on yours.

YOUR QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

Which ID is safest to upload to Instagram?+

A passport or national ID with a clear photo and the name you use on the account.

Can I upload ID more than once?+

You can usually retry, but wait and fix the cause first. Many quick retries make it harder.

Should I send my ID to someone who says they work for Instagram?+

No. Upload ID only inside the Instagram app or website.

Will Instagram share my ID with others?+

Instagram says IDs are stored securely and aren’t shown to other users. Read the help page for the current retention details.

Do I need a government ID?+

Not always. If you have none, Instagram accepts two non-government documents, as long as the details match and at least one shows your photo or date of birth.

Is it safe to send my ID to someone who offers to help?+

No. Only upload to Instagram’s own screen. A helper never needs your ID to be sent to them.

How long does Instagram take to check my ID?+

Meta does not publish a timeline, and it varies. Keep your confirmation, check your email including spam, and avoid submitting again unless Instagram asks you to.

Should I submit my ID more than once?+

Only if Instagram asks for it. Repeated uploads of the same document do not move things along and can look like spam.

Is an ID request the same as a video selfie?+

No. An ID request asks for a document photo, while a video selfie asks you to move your face in front of the camera. Follow the one shown on your screen.
