Instagram video selfie verification: what to expect

The video selfie is now a standard step in many Instagram recovery and appeal flows. Doing it right the first time saves days of waiting.

Why Instagram asks for it

Instagram uses a video selfie to confirm that a real person is behind an account and, where the account has photos of the owner, that the person matches. It appears in hacked account recovery, some suspension appeals and checks for suspicious activity. Instagram’s Help Center explains that the video is used for verification and not shown on your profile.

How it works

  1. Instagram prompts you to record a short video.
  2. You follow on-screen guidance to turn your head in different directions.
  3. The video is submitted for review.
  4. You are told the result later, often by email.

Tips to pass the first time

  • Use good, even lighting and face a window or lamp.
  • Remove sunglasses, hats and anything covering your face.
  • Hold the phone at eye level and keep it steady.
  • Follow the prompts slowly and fully.
  • Use the device you normally use for Instagram.
  • Make sure the account actually has photos of you if it is your personal account; if not, Instagram may use other checks.

If the account has no photos of you

Business, meme or theme accounts often have no photos of the owner. Instagram may then ask other questions, such as the email or phone used to sign up and the device type. Answer accurately.

If it fails or gets stuck

  • Wait for the result before retrying; repeated submissions can slow things down.
  • Check the email on the account, including spam.
  • Restart from instagram.com/hacked if the flow breaks.
  • Update the app and try on stable Wi-Fi.

Privacy

Only record the selfie inside the official Instagram app or website. Never send a selfie video to someone claiming to be from Meta through DMs or email.

Common problems and fixes

  • The camera won’t start – check the app has camera permission in your phone settings and close other camera apps.
  • “We couldn’t confirm your identity” – the result may be final for that attempt; check whether a retry or another method is offered.
  • Upload stuck at 99% – switch to stable Wi-Fi, update the app and try again later rather than repeatedly.
  • No response for days – reviews have no published timeframe; keep checking the email on the account, including spam.

Old accounts and changed appearance

If the photos on the account are many years old, or you look very different now, verification can be harder. Make sure your face is clearly visible and well lit. For accounts that feature several people, the system needs to match the owner, so personal accounts with clear photos of the owner tend to be easier to verify than group or brand accounts.

How this protects you

Video selfies make it harder for attackers to take over accounts with stolen passwords alone, and harder for scammers to fake ownership claims. It is an inconvenience when you are locked out, but it is also one of the reasons a stolen account can be recovered at all.
