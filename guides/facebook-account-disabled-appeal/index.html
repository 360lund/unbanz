Facebook account disabled or suspended: how to appeal

Short answer: use the “Disagree” or appeal option on the notice, confirm your identity, and submit once before the deadline shown on it.

Facebook gives most suspended accounts a window to disagree. You can do it yourself; here is how.

Suspended or disabled?

Facebook’s Help Center page My personal Facebook account is suspended or disabled explains the two notices:

  • We suspended your account: you can appeal, usually within 180 days. Nobody can see or use the account meanwhile.
  • We disabled your account: the permanent stage, after the window ends or an appeal fails. You can’t request another review.

The window varies by region, so trust the date on your own screen. Meta may also disable an account without suspending it first for severe violations.

Before you start

  • Screenshot the notice, including the date and any policy named.
  • Make sure you can receive codes on the email or phone linked to the account.
  • Have a government ID ready and check the name matches the profile.
  • Download your information if the notice offers it.

How to appeal, step by step

  1. Check it really is a suspension. If your password or email changed without you, it is a hack: go to facebook.com/hacked instead.
  2. Log in on your usual device and network and open the notice.
  3. Tap Disagree with decision (or the appeal option shown).
  4. Confirm your identity. Enter the code, take the video selfie or upload ID if asked.
  5. Add a short note if there is a box: you own the account, how you use it, and why the reason does not fit. Mention a hack if there was one.
  6. Submit once and wait for the confirmation screen.
  7. Watch your email, including spam, and the app. Facebook does not publish a review time.

Why Facebook suspends accounts

The help page lists content that breaks the Community Standards, misrepresenting your identity (including impersonation), and contacting people to harass or promote in ways that are not allowed. Some regions also apply age rules. Hacked accounts are sometimes suspended for activity the owner did not do, and automated systems make mistakes.

Pages, groups and ads

A disabled profile can affect Pages and ad accounts where you are the only admin. A Page with no one left in full control is deactivated and scheduled for deletion, so add a trusted second admin now. See restricted Facebook Page and Meta ad account restricted.

What to avoid

  • Letting the window run out. After it ends you can’t request another review.
  • Making a second profile. Meta disables accounts created to get around restrictions.
  • Appeal “email addresses” from forums. They are not an appeal route. Use the notice.
  • Sharing your password or codes, or replying to “Meta support” messages on Messenger. Facebook does not run appeals that way.

YOUR QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

How long do I have to appeal on Facebook?+

You have 180 days to disagree with the decision. After that, the account is permanently disabled.

Can I get my Facebook data while suspended?+

Often, yes. Check the notice for an option to download your information.

Should I create a new Facebook account?+

No. Meta may disable accounts made to get around restrictions, which makes things worse.

What happens if I miss the 180-day appeal window?+

Facebook says the account is then permanently disabled and you can’t request another review of the decision.

Can I appeal again after my Facebook appeal is rejected?+

Facebook’s help page says that if your appeal isn’t successful, the account is permanently disabled and you won’t be able to request another review.

Will my Facebook Page be deleted if my profile is disabled?+

Not automatically, but a Page with no remaining person who has full control is deactivated and scheduled for deletion. Make sure someone else you trust has full control.

Does Unbanz need my Facebook login?+

No. Send the profile link and the notice. Never share your password, login codes or recovery codes with anyone.

How long does a Facebook appeal take?+

Facebook doesn’t publish a fixed timeframe. Submit once, then watch your email and the Facebook app.
