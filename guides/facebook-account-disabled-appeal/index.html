Facebook account disabled or suspended: how to appeal

Facebook gives most suspended accounts a window to disagree before the decision becomes permanent. Here is how that process works and how to use it well.

Suspended vs permanently disabled

According to Facebook’s Help Center page on suspended or disabled personal accounts, when Facebook suspends an account you will see a message when you log in, and you have 180 days to disagree with the decision. If you do not disagree within that time, or if your appeal is unsuccessful, the account is permanently disabled. While suspended, the account is not visible to others and you cannot use Facebook.

Some notices say the account is already disabled and that the decision cannot be reversed. In those cases there may be no appeal route available.

Why Facebook suspends accounts

Facebook acts on accounts that go against the Meta Community Standards or its Terms. Meta’s page on disabling accounts explains that violations generally add up as strikes, that repeated violations lead to longer restrictions, and that some severe violations can lead to removal after one occurrence. It also lists accounts created to get around restrictions and accounts that misrepresent identity – for example, fake names or impersonation – as reasons for disabling.

Common real-world triggers include using a name that does not match your identity, running multiple personal profiles, suspicious login activity, sending lots of friend requests or messages quickly, and content flagged as spam or scams.

How to disagree with the decision

  1. Log in to Facebook on the app or at facebook.com with the suspended account.
  2. On the suspension notice, choose the option to disagree with the decision or request a review.
  3. Follow the on-screen steps. Facebook may ask you to confirm your identity, for example with a photo or video selfie, a security code sent to your phone or email, or an ID document.
  4. Submit the request once, then wait for a decision. Notifications usually arrive at the email or phone on the account.

Only upload ID inside Facebook’s own flow. Meta’s Help Center lists which ID types it accepts and explains how it handles them.

If you think the account was hacked

If the account was suspended after someone else got in – for example, scam posts you did not make – say so in the appeal and use Facebook’s official recovery tool at facebook.com/hacked. Secure the email address linked to the account first so the attacker cannot reset it again.

Tips for a stronger appeal

  • Act early in the 180-day window; do not wait until the end.
  • Make sure the name on the profile matches your ID if you are asked to verify.
  • Use the same device and location you normally log in from where possible.
  • Do not open a new personal account to get around the suspension – Meta may disable it as well.
  • Keep screenshots of the notice, dates and any reference numbers.

Pages, groups and business assets

A suspended personal profile can also cut you off from Pages and Business tools you admin. If those matter, add a trusted second admin to important assets in advance. If an ad account or Business Portfolio was restricted, that is a separate process – see our guide on Meta ad account restrictions.

Before you submit

Because you usually get one chance to disagree, preparation pays off:

  • Screenshot the notice, including the date and any policy mentioned.
  • Check you can receive codes on the email or phone linked to the account.
  • Have a government-issued ID ready in case you are asked, and confirm the name matches the profile.
  • Download a copy of your information if the notice offers it – it is useful even if the appeal fails.
  • Write two or three factual sentences about how you use Facebook and why you believe the decision is wrong.

Afterwards, check the email on the account regularly, including spam. Ignore messages from “Meta support” pages or profiles that contact you on Messenger asking for passwords, codes or payment – Facebook does not run appeals that way.

If the account is linked to a business, think ahead: an important Page or Business Portfolio should never depend on a single personal profile. Add at least one other trusted admin so the business keeps running if one profile is restricted.

YOUR QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

How long do I have to appeal a suspended Facebook account?+

Facebook's Help Center says you have 180 days to disagree with the decision. After that, or if the appeal is unsuccessful, the account is permanently disabled.

Can I see my Facebook data while suspended?+

Facebook may let you download a copy of your information during the suspension period from the notice. Check the options shown when you log in.

Will creating a new Facebook account help?+

No. Meta says accounts created to get around restrictions may be disabled, which can make the situation worse.
