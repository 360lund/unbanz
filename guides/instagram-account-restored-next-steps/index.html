Your Instagram account is back: what to do in the first week

Short answer: secure the account first: change the password, check logins, confirm contact details and turn on two-factor authentication. Then review what changed and post gently.

Getting an account back is only half the job. The same weakness that let someone in, or the same activity that got you restricted, can cause a repeat if you carry on as before.

What to do first

Don’t post, follow or message anyone yet. Take ten minutes to lock things down. If the account was hacked, assume whoever had it may still have the email or a saved login somewhere.

Step 1: Change the password and sign out other devices

Set a new, unique password in Accounts Center under Password and security, and use the sessions list to log out everywhere else. Do it on a device you trust.

Step 2: Check email, phone and linked accounts

Open Contact info and make sure the email and phone are yours. Review linked accounts and remove third-party apps you don’t recognise. If the email linked is not yours, change it and secure that mailbox too.

Step 3: Turn on two-factor authentication

Use an authentication app and save backup codes somewhere safe. See our guide on lost two-factor access for what to do if you’re ever locked out.

Step 4: Review what changed

  • Look through recent posts, Stories, bio and links for edits you didn’t make.
  • Check sent messages and the accounts you follow.
  • Open Account Status for removed content or feature limits.
  • Warn followers about any suspicious messages sent from your account.

Step 5: Ease back in

If the account was restricted for activity, return slowly. Post normally, avoid bulk follows, likes or comments, and don’t reuse anything that was removed.

Why a restored account can fail again

If the account was hacked, the attacker may have tried to leave a way back in: a connected app, a forwarding rule on the email, or a saved device session. If it was restricted for activity, the same behaviour, such as rapid follows or repeated links, will trigger the same system again. Thinking about the cause is more useful than any single setting.

Keep a note of the date it was restored and what you changed. If something odd happens in the next few weeks, you’ll know where to start looking.

What not to do

  • Don’t publicly announce details of how you got it back in a way that invites scammers.
  • Don’t reconnect suspicious apps or tools.
  • Don’t assume the problem is over without checking Account Status.

Frequently asked questions

Can the account be banned again right after recovery?+

It can if the underlying cause continues. Review what happened and adjust.

Should I tell followers?+

If the account sent messages or posts you didn’t make, yes, so they don’t click bad links.

Does my reach return straight away?+

Meta doesn’t publish a timeline for any recovery of reach, so be patient and consistent.
