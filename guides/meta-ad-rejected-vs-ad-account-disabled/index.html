Meta ad rejected vs ad account disabled: what's the difference and what to do

“Ad rejected” and “ad account disabled” both stop your campaigns, but they are very different problems. One is about a single ad. The other is about whether you can advertise at all. Treating one like the other is one of the most common and costly mistakes advertisers make.

The short version

  • Ad rejected: one ad didn't pass review. Your account still works. You can edit the ad, create a new one, or request another review.
  • Ad account disabled or restricted: the account (or the person, Page or business portfolio behind it) can't run ads. You fix it with a review of the account in Business Support Home, not by editing ads.

When an ad is rejected

Meta's rejected ad help page says that when an ad is rejected, you'll receive an email and a link to Business Support Home showing the reason. Meta lists the main reasons as ads that break the law in the advertiser's jurisdiction, are discriminatory, promote deceptive or misleading practices meant to scam people, or don't follow the Community Standards, Advertising Standards or Commerce Policies.

You then have three options:

  1. Edit the ad in Ads Manager or Business Suite. It's automatically resubmitted for review.
  2. Create a new ad that complies. Meta warns that if you delete the rejected ad, you may lose your ability to request another review.
  3. Request another review in Business Support Home if you believe the rejection was wrong. Meta says it aims to decide within 48 hours, relies more heavily on human reviewers for re-reviews, and that a successfully overturned rejection “will not count as an ad violation.”

Two details that matter later:

  • Meta says editing or deleting a rejected ad does not remove the violation from your account.
  • If a re-review upholds the rejection, the ad stays rejected and you can't request another review for the same ad.

When an ad account is disabled or restricted

Meta says that for severe or repeated ad violations it can go beyond rejecting ads and restrict your business account, ad account, Page or user account from advertising. Its Advertising Standards say a restricted account or asset can't be used to advertise.

Payment problems are a separate cause; see our guide to ad accounts disabled for payment issues.

To request a review, Meta's restricted advertising page says an admin should go to Business Support Home › Account status overview › the restricted account › What you can do › Request review, then follow the steps. Meta says requesting another review won't hurt your standing.

How rejections turn into a disabled account

A string of rejections is often what leads to a restricted account. The pattern we see most is:

  1. An ad is rejected.
  2. The advertiser deletes it and uploads a near-identical version.
  3. That is rejected too, and the violations accumulate even though each ad was deleted.
  4. The account is restricted for repeated violations.

Meta's own wording backs this up: violations remain on the account even if you edit or delete the ad, and repeated violations can lead to restrictions.

What to do in each case

If the rejection was wrong

Request a review rather than deleting the ad. An overturned rejection doesn't count as a violation.

If the rejection was right

Fix the specific component, such as the image, text, targeting or landing page, before resubmitting. Don't just reword slightly and hope.

If the account is restricted

Stop creating new ads or ad accounts. Find the highest restricted item in Business Support Home, whether that's the portfolio, the ad account, a Page or a person, and request a review there first. Our guides to Meta ad account restrictions and Business Portfolio restrictions go into detail.

Keeping rejections from adding up

  • Check new campaigns against the Advertising Standards before publishing, especially in health, finance, dating and regulated goods.
  • Make sure the ad and landing page say the same thing.
  • Use request-review for genuine mistakes; fix and resubmit for genuine problems.
  • Keep a simple log of rejections so you can see a pattern before Meta does.

A quick triage checklist

Ask three questions. Can you still create a new ad in this account? If yes, it's an ad-level problem. Does Business Support Home show the account, a Page or a person as restricted? If yes, it's an account-level problem. Is there a payment or billing warning? If yes, fix payment first.

YOUR QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

Does a rejected ad count against my ad account?+

It can. Meta says editing or deleting a rejected ad does not remove the violation, and severe or repeated violations can lead to advertising restrictions. A rejection overturned on review does not count.

How long does Meta take to re-review a rejected ad?+

Meta says it aims to review and decide within 48 hours.

Should I delete a rejected ad?+

Not if you want to appeal. Meta warns you may lose the ability to request another review if you delete the ad.

Can I request more than one review for the same ad?+

No. Meta says if an ad is rejected again after a re-review, you won't be able to submit another review request for that ad.
