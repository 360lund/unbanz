The rules behind every decision

Instagram enforces its Community Guidelines and Terms of Use, which sit alongside Meta’s wider Community Standards. Enforcement is a mix of automated systems and human review. Most of the time it starts with removing content or limiting features; account-level action comes later or for serious violations.

How strikes work

Meta’s Transparency Center explains that violations generally count as strikes. More strikes lead to longer restrictions on creating content, and after repeated violations an account may be removed depending on severity and frequency. Some violations are severe enough to disable an account after a single occurrence – Meta gives child sexual exploitation as an example. You can see recent violations and restrictions in the app under Settings > Account Status.

The most common reasons

Spam and inauthentic behaviour

The guidelines ask you to foster meaningful interactions. Instagram’s Help Center mentions examples such as repeatedly contacting people for commercial purposes without consent, artificially collecting likes, followers or shares, and posting repetitive content. Bulk following, mass DMs and engagement pods can all look like this.

Third-party apps and shared logins

Growth and automation tools that log in with your password create activity that looks automated and can expose your account. Instagram warns against sharing your login with these services.

Impersonation and fake identity

Accounts that pretend to be someone else, or misrepresent who runs them, can be disabled. Meta also says it may disable accounts created to get around earlier restrictions.

Intellectual property

Repeatedly posting content you do not have rights to – music, video clips, photos or brand assets – can lead to removal after repeated copyright or trademark reports.

Harmful or restricted content

Hate speech, bullying and harassment, violent or graphic content, nudity and sexual content, and the sale of regulated goods are covered in detail in the Community Standards. Even content posted as a joke can be enforced.

Age

You must be at least 13 to use Instagram (older in some countries). Accounts reported as underage may be asked to verify age.

Mistakes happen

Automated systems are not perfect. Sudden spikes in activity, a hacked account posting spam, or a mass-report campaign against you can lead to action you disagree with. That is why the review option on the notice matters – see how to appeal a suspension and what to do when your account is disabled.

How to protect your account

  • Turn on two-factor authentication and keep your email and phone current.
  • Stop using apps that need your Instagram password.
  • Grow gradually; avoid bursts of follows, likes or identical messages.
  • Post content you own or have permission to use, and credit sources.
  • Check Account Status regularly and delete content that has been flagged.
  • Do not buy followers, likes or comments.

Using Account Status to stay ahead

Account Status is the most underused tool on Instagram. It shows content that was removed, whether your account is at risk of being disabled, and whether features or recommendations are limited. Check it after any warning notification.

  • If content was removed and you believe it was a mistake, many removals can be reviewed from Account Status.
  • If the account is “at risk”, slow down: pause automation, avoid mass messaging and review recent posts against the guidelines.
  • If your content is not eligible to be recommended, it will not appear on Explore or Reels to non-followers until the issue clears.

Treat repeated warnings as a signal to change behaviour, not just to delete posts. Strikes can add up over time, and the history on the account is part of how future decisions are made.

For businesses and creators with teams, agree internal rules on who can post, what music and footage you are licensed to use, and which tools can connect to the account. Most avoidable bans come from a single team member or tool doing something the account owner did not know about.

YOUR QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

How many violations before a ban?+

There is no fixed number. Strikes add up to longer restrictions, and one severe violation can disable an account.

Can mass reporting ban an account?+

Reports trigger reviews, but decisions rest on the rules, not the report count.

Where can I see my violations?+

Open Settings, then Account Status, to see removed content and current restrictions.
