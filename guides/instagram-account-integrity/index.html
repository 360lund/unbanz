Instagram suspended for “account integrity”: what it means

“Account integrity” is one of the vaguest reasons on a suspension notice. It refers to rules about authentic identity and behaviour, not about a specific post.

What account integrity covers

Meta’s Community Standards include a section on account integrity and authentic identity. In short, Meta wants accounts to represent real people or organisations, to be used authentically, and not to be used to mislead others or get around enforcement. A suspension for account integrity means the system or a reviewer concluded the account broke those rules – it often does not point to one post.

Common triggers

  • Creating or using an account to get around a previous restriction or disabled account.
  • Using a fake name or identity, or impersonating someone else.
  • Operating several accounts in a coordinated way to boost reach.
  • Automated or scripted activity – bots, mass following, mass messaging.
  • Buying, selling or renting accounts, usernames or engagement.
  • Sharing login access widely, especially with services that act on your behalf.
  • Linked signals: shared devices, phone numbers or payment details with accounts already actioned.

Instagram and Facebook together

Because Meta links accounts through Accounts Center, an account integrity action can affect connected Instagram and Facebook profiles. If both are affected, appeal each one through its own notice and keep your details consistent.

How to appeal

  1. Log in and choose the option to disagree with the decision.
  2. Complete identity verification carefully – integrity cases often hinge on proving a real, single owner.
  3. Where you can write, explain that the account is yours, how you use it, and anything that might look inauthentic but is not (for example, a shared business device).
  4. Remove automation tools and stop using any service that logs in for you.

What not to do

  • Do not create a new account while the case is open.
  • Do not buy “unban” services that ask for your password.
  • Do not submit fake or edited ID – that is itself an integrity violation.

Keeping your account authentic

Use one personal account per person, keep your name and details honest, avoid growth tools, and keep two-factor authentication on. For brands, make clear in the bio who runs the account.

Examples that look inauthentic but aren’t

Plenty of genuine accounts trip integrity signals. Some common innocent explanations:

  • A family sharing one phone or tablet between several personal accounts.
  • A social media manager logging into many client accounts from one laptop.
  • A user travelling and logging in from several countries in a week.
  • A business that bought a phone number previously used by someone else.
  • A person who uses a nickname or stage name rather than their legal name.

If one of these applies to you, mention it briefly and factually when you have the chance. Reviewers can only weigh information they have. For agencies, using Meta’s official business tools to manage access – rather than sharing passwords – reduces this risk considerably.

Why “evasion” matters so much

Meta’s enforcement policy specifically lists accounts created to get around restrictions as something it disables. That is why opening a backup account during an integrity case is so risky: it can be read as confirmation of the very behaviour the original decision was about, and both accounts can end up disabled. Wait for the outcome, and if you need a public presence meanwhile, use your website or other platforms.

Can an account integrity suspension be reversed?+

Sometimes, if the review finds the account is authentic. Complete verification carefully and explain any innocent signals.

It can, because Meta links accounts through Accounts Center. Each affected account has its own notice and appeal.
