Snapchat device ban (SS06): what it means and whether you can appeal

Short answer: an SS06 message means the device is restricted from Snapchat. If the login screen shows Appeal Decision, use it once. A newly purchased device usually won’t be unbanned.

A device ban is different from an account ban. It’s linked to the phone, so a new account on the same phone can hit the same wall. Snapchat’s help page explains when an appeal is possible.

What to do first

Read the exact message on the screen. Write down the error code and whether it mentions an account, a device or a policy. If the login screen offers Appeal Decision, that’s your route. If it doesn’t, an appeal isn’t available from there.

Step 1: Confirm it’s a device ban

Snapchat’s help page describes error SS06 as a restriction on the device. It means Snapchat has limited that phone’s access, not necessarily that your own username broke the rules.

Step 2: Use Appeal Decision if offered

On the login screen, choose Appeal Decision and follow the prompts. Give accurate information and submit once. Snapchat doesn’t publish a timeline for decisions.

Step 3: If you bought the device used

Snapchat’s help page notes that devices bought recently are generally not unbanned. If you suspect the phone came with a ban, check with the seller, and report any problem to them. Also try the same account on a different device you own.

Step 4: Check your own account separately

Try your username on another phone or the web to see whether the account itself is locked or limited. That tells you which problem you have.

Why device-level restrictions exist

Snapchat applies some restrictions to the device so that someone whose account has been removed can’t simply start again on the same phone. It’s also why a second-hand phone can arrive with a problem. Snapchat’s terms don’t allow unofficial apps and running several accounts to get around enforcement, and those are the kinds of behaviour that can lead to device-level action. Keeping to one account, using the official app and not buying or selling accounts keeps you well clear.

Before you buy a used phone

Open Snapchat on the phone in the shop, if the seller allows it, and try logging in. If you see an error, don’t buy it. It’s a thirty-second test that avoids a problem you can’t easily fix afterwards.

What not to do

  • Don’t try to hide or change device identifiers. That’s a way of dodging the ban and can lead to further action.
  • Don’t buy a “ban remover.” It’s a scam.
  • Don’t submit multiple appeals in a row.

YOUR QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

Is SS06 permanent?+

Snapchat’s help page says appeal is only available if the login screen offers it. Otherwise it’s a final decision.

Can I use my account on another phone?+

If the account isn’t banned, usually yes. Check the message you see.

Does a factory reset remove the ban?+

No. Snapchat’s help page doesn’t suggest it will.
