Instagram account suspended: how to appeal

Short answer: tap the appeal option on the suspension notice, complete any identity check, and submit once before the deadline on your screen. Then watch your email and the app.

You can do this yourself in a few minutes. The steps below follow Instagram’s own flow.

Before you start

  • Screenshot the full notice, including the deadline and any policy named.
  • Make sure you can receive codes on the email and phone linked to the account.
  • Use your usual phone, the latest app version and your normal network.
  • Stop any automation or scheduling tool that logs in with your password.

How to appeal, step by step

  1. Log in. Open the Instagram app or instagram.com and log in to the suspended account. The appeal option only shows once you are logged in.
  2. Read the notice. Note the reason given and the number of days left.
  3. Tap the appeal option. It is usually labelled Appeal or Disagree with decision. Go through every screen until you see a confirmation.
  4. Confirm it is you. You may be asked for a code or a video selfie. Do the selfie in good light with nothing covering your face. Instagram’s video selfie help page explains the check.
  5. Add a short note if there is a box. Two or three factual sentences are enough (see below).
  6. Wait. Check the email on the account, including spam, and the app. Instagram does not publish a review time.

What to write

If you get a text box, keep it short and factual:

  • You own the account and how you use it (personal, creator, business).
  • Why the reason given does not fit, for example a misread post or a login that was not you.
  • Anything you already removed or fixed.

No threats, templates, all-caps or long emotional messages. Never include your password or codes.

The deadline

The countdown on your screen is a deadline to appeal, not a waiting period. The account does not come back by itself when it ends. Facebook’s Help Center describes the same window for suspended accounts: you can appeal within 180 days, and if you don’t, or the appeal fails, the account is permanently disabled. Instagram does not print one fixed number for every account, so go by your own notice and appeal early.

What to avoid

  • Waiting. Appeal early so verification is not rushed.
  • Making a new account. Meta disables accounts created to get around restrictions.
  • Stopping halfway. Only a confirmation screen means the appeal was sent.
  • Appeal links from DMs or emails. Fake appeal pages steal logins. Start from the app or help.instagram.com.
  • Sharing your login. No genuine helper needs your password or codes.

Why accounts get suspended

Common reasons named on notices include impersonation, hateful conduct, threats, graphic content and spam-like or inauthentic activity. Some suspensions come from automated systems, and some follow a hack. See why Instagram bans accounts and the Community Guidelines.

After a decision

Approved: remove what likely triggered it (automation apps, bought engagement, repeated messages) and review your security settings.

Rejected: Meta treats the decision as final and the in-app option usually disappears. If the account was hacked before the suspension, try instagram.com/hacked. In the EU, the notice may mention out-of-court dispute bodies under the Digital Services Act. Our disabled Instagram account guide covers the other options.

Login and recovery questions (2026)

What if I no longer have the email or phone on the account?

Start from facebook.com/login/identify or the Instagram “Forgot password” screen on a phone or computer you have logged in from before. Meta may offer other options there, such as a code to a different contact, a video selfie or an ID photo. Which options appear depends on the account.

Does a recognised device help?

Often, yes. Meta may trust a phone, browser or network you have used to log in before, so try recovery from that device and connection rather than a new one.

YOUR QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

What should I write in the disagree box?+

Two or three plain sentences: who you are, that you did not break the rule named, and any hack or travel detail that explains the flag. Skip long essays.

Does changing my username after a suspension help?+

You usually cannot change it while suspended. Creating a new account on the same phone often makes things worse.

Can family submit the appeal for me?+

The person who owns the account needs to complete ID or selfie checks. Someone else can help gather screenshots, not take the selfie.

How many times can I disagree with an Instagram suspension?+

Usually once per decision. A second request rarely appears and does not speed anything up, so make the first one accurate.

Does the 180-day countdown pause after I appeal?+

The counter is shown until Instagram decides. What matters is that the appeal was sent inside the window.

What happens to my posts during a suspension?+

They are hidden from others while the account is suspended and come back if the suspension is lifted.

How many times can I appeal a suspension?+

Usually once per decision. Make that first appeal complete and accurate.

Do I need to send ID to appeal?+

Sometimes. Instagram may ask for a video selfie. Only verify inside the official app or website.

What if I miss the appeal deadline?+

The account may be permanently disabled and the appeal option removed.

Does my Instagram account come back automatically after 180 days?+

Nothing in Meta’s help pages says so. The number shown is a deadline to appeal. Meta’s Facebook Help Center says that if you don’t appeal within the window, or the appeal fails, the account is permanently disabled.

How long does an Instagram suspension appeal take?+

Instagram doesn’t publish a fixed timeframe. Keep an eye on the email linked to the account and on the app after you submit.

Why does Instagram ask for a video selfie?+

To check that a real person owns the account. Instagram says you turn your head in different directions, you won’t have access while it’s reviewed, and you may be able to resubmit if it isn’t accepted.

Is it safe to use an Instagram appeal link someone sent me?+

Be very careful. Start from the Instagram app or help.instagram.com instead, and never enter your password or codes on a page someone sent you in a DM.
