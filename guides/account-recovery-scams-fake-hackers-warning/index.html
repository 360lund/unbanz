Account recovery scams: how fake “hackers” and fake “support agents” work

Short answer: any helper who asks for your password, a code, an upfront payment in gift cards or crypto, or claims to have special access to your account is a scam. Recovery only happens through official routes.

When an account is locked or hacked, the person who lost it is under pressure and looking for help. Scammers know that, and they find victims in comments, DMs and search ads within hours.

What to do first

Pause before you reply to any offer of help you didn’t ask for. Then check the official route for your platform, such as the Instagram, Facebook or Snapchat help pages. If you’ve already contacted someone, stop sending information and read the warning signs below.

Step 1: Know the warning signs

  • They contacted you first, often in comments or by DM.
  • They ask for your password, a login code, or access to your email.
  • They ask for payment in gift cards, crypto or by transfer before doing anything.
  • They claim to have an inside contact or a special tool.
  • They say the account will be deleted unless you act now.
  • They push you to move to Telegram or WhatsApp.

Step 2: Understand how real recovery works

Genuine recovery uses the platform’s own forms: login help, identity checks with ID or a video selfie, and appeals inside the app. No outside party can force a platform’s decision. Anyone promising that they can is offering something that doesn’t exist.

Step 3: Check any helper, including us

Ask what exactly they’ll do, and whether they need your password. A legitimate adviser explains the official route, doesn’t ask for your login, doesn’t claim a connection inside the platform and doesn’t promise outcomes. Be wary of anything that sounds too good.

Step 4: If you’ve been scammed

Change the passwords of the account and its email, log out other sessions, and contact your bank about payments. Report the scammer to the platform and local authorities. Save screenshots of the conversation.

A typical scam conversation

It begins with a comment under your post or a DM from an account with a name like “Account Recovery Expert”, offering to get the account back. The first request is small, perhaps a screenshot, then your username, then “verification” through a code sent to your phone, which is really the login code for your own account. Next comes a fee in crypto or gift cards. After payment, there’s a second fee for “release”. Each step feels reasonable because the previous one cost you something. Recognising that escalation pattern is the best defence.

What not to do

  • Don’t give anyone your login or codes.
  • Don’t pay to “unlock” a recovery.
  • Don’t be embarrassed. These scams are designed to catch careful people at a stressful moment.

Frequently asked questions

Can anyone at Meta help me for a fee?+

No. Anyone who says so is not telling the truth. Use the official forms.

Are all recovery services scams?+

Not all, but the ones that ask for your password, promise results, or claim inside access are.

What should a safe adviser do?+

Explain the official route, ask for no passwords and promise no outcomes.
