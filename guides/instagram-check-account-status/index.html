Instagram Account Status: how to read it and what each notice means

Short answer: open Settings and tap Account Status. It lists removed content, feature restrictions and, for eligible accounts, whether your content can be recommended.

Many Instagram problems are announced quietly in one screen. Before you panic, check Account Status. It is the closest thing to a dashboard for rule violations.

What to do first

Open Instagram, go to your profile, tap the menu, then Settings and look for Account Status. It can also appear under account support or help. If you can’t find it, search “account status” in Settings.

Take a screenshot of everything that appears before you click anything. It’s a record of what Instagram said and when.

Step 1: Read the removed content list

If Instagram has taken down a post, Reel, Story or comment, it appears under removed content. Tap the item to see which policy applied. If the screen offers a review, you will see a button for it. Not every item can be reviewed, and Meta doesn’t publish a timeline.

Step 2: Check feature limits

Account Status also shows if a feature, such as going live or messaging new people, has been limited. These limits are usually temporary and are tied to specific violations. Read the date and the reason given.

Step 3: Check recommendation eligibility

If you have a public professional account, Account Status can show whether your content is eligible to be recommended in places such as Explore, Reels and Suggested accounts. If it isn’t, Instagram points to the content or profile element that caused it and lets you edit it or request a review. Eligibility doesn’t promise any amount of reach.

Step 4: Decide your next move

  • Content removed in error: request a review from that screen.
  • A strike: read our guide on how strikes add up and expire.
  • Account disabled: use the appeal route and our disabled-account guide.
  • Nothing listed but reach is down: see our reach guide.

Four common results, and what to do

  • “Your content was removed.” Open the item, read the policy, and request a review only if you believe it was a mistake.
  • “You can’t use this feature.” Read the end date. Avoid the activity that triggered it, such as mass following, until the limit lifts.
  • “Your content isn’t eligible to be recommended.” Look for the item named and remove or edit it, or request a review where offered.
  • Everything is clear. Then the problem is elsewhere: a login issue, a reach issue or an outage. Check Instagram’s status and your Insights.

Keep your own record

Screenshots of Account Status, with the date, build a record you can use later. If your account is ever restricted, a timeline showing earlier removals, limits and appeals makes your next appeal easier to write and helps you see patterns, such as a certain type of post that keeps being flagged.

Make it a monthly habit

Open Account Status on the first of each month and note what it says. Write down the date and any removed content. A quick routine means you see a problem within days instead of discovering weeks later that a feature was limited.

For team accounts, add the check to the same list as posting schedules and give one person responsibility for it.

If you can’t open Account Status at all

Account Status only works while you can still log in. If Instagram shows a full-screen notice instead of your feed, the problem is bigger than a feature limit and the screen you need is a different one.

  • “Your account has been suspended” with a countdown: you have a limited window to disagree with the decision. Follow the steps in our suspended account appeal guide before the deadline passes.
  • “Your account has been disabled”: the account is no longer visible to others. See Instagram account disabled for the routes that still exist.
  • A request for a video selfie or ID: Instagram wants to confirm you are a real person or the owner. See video selfie verification and ID verification requests.
  • You were logged out and the password no longer works: this points to a hacked account, not a rule violation. Start with hacked Instagram account recovery.

In each of these situations, take screenshots of every screen and every email from Instagram before you tap anything. The wording tells you which route applies.

What not to do

  • Don’t delete posts in a panic. Removing evidence can complicate a later appeal.
  • Don’t file the same request repeatedly.
  • Don’t assume a clean Account Status means every issue is gone. Some limits come from automated checks that don’t show up.

YOUR QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

Where is Account Status on Instagram?+

It’s in Settings. If it’s not visible, search for it in the Settings search bar or look under account support.

Does Account Status show shadowbans?+

Instagram doesn’t use that term. It shows recommendation eligibility for eligible accounts, which is the closest official equivalent.

Will every violation appear there?+

Not necessarily. Notices can also arrive by notification or email, so check both.

Why can’t I see Account Status after my account was suspended?+

Account Status needs an active login. A suspended or disabled account shows a separate notice with its own options, so follow the steps on that screen instead.

Does a clean Account Status mean my account is safe?+

It means nothing is listed right now. Automated limits and security checks can still apply, so keep an eye on notifications and emails from Instagram as well.

Can someone fix my Account Status for me?+

No one outside Meta can edit Account Status. Where a review option is offered, you or someone helping you can prepare a clear request, but Instagram makes the final decision.
