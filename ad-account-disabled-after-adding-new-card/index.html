Ad account disabled after adding a new card?

Changing a payment method is one of the moments Meta watches closely. This page is for advertisers whose ad account was restricted or disabled soon after adding or switching a card.

Updated 29 October 2026

Message us on WhatsApp ↗︎Prefer email? ↗︎

A new card can trigger a restriction when Meta sees it as a change in who controls the account, or when the card is linked to a restricted account. Check the card details, remove any card you do not recognise, then request a review once. If it fails, Unbanz can prepare the appeal.

Why timing matters

Meta checks account changes, and a new card, new admin or new country together can look like a takeover.

Check the card

Name, billing address and expiry must be correct. A prepaid or virtual card shared across accounts is often a problem.

Check for a takeover

If you did not add the card, treat it as a hack. Secure the profile and remove the card before anything else.

Why a new card can trigger a restriction

  • The card was used on another ad account that Meta restricted or disabled.
  • The same card is attached to many ad accounts that look unrelated.
  • A virtual or prepaid card was declined, which counts as a failed charge.
  • Someone else added the card after getting into an admin profile.
  • The billing country on the card does not match the business country you set.

What to do

  1. Open Billing and payments and see which card is attached and who added it.
  2. If you do not recognise it, remove it, change the admin passwords and turn on two-factor, then read our guide on hacked ad accounts.
  3. If it is yours, make sure the name and address match the business and the card is a normal debit or credit card.
  4. Clear any pending charge.
  5. Request a review in Account Quality and say plainly what you changed and why.

What to avoid

Do not rotate through several new cards hoping one will work. Each failed attempt is another signal, and it can spread the restriction to more accounts. Do not send card details to anyone offering to help.

How Unbanz helps

We read the notice and the timeline, work out whether the card or something around it is the likely trigger, and prepare the appeal. We never need card numbers. You can compare notice types on our page on restricted versus disabled.

What to send

  • The ad account ID and the business portfolio ID.
  • A screenshot of the notice in Account Quality, with the date it appeared.
  • What was running: the product or offer, the landing page and roughly how much was being spent per day.
  • Anything that changed shortly before: a new card, a new admin, a new domain, a login from another country.
  • When you added or changed the card, and who did it.

Please do not send passwords, login codes, card numbers or access tokens.

Good to know.

Can a new payment method get my ad account disabled?+

It can. A card tied to a restricted account, or a change that looks like a takeover, can lead to a restriction.

Should I remove the card?+

If you did not add it, yes, and secure the profile first. If it is yours and correct, leave it and request a review.

Does a virtual card cause problems?+

Some virtual and prepaid cards are declined or shared across accounts, which Meta can treat as a risk. A standard card in the business name is safer.

Will Unbanz ask for my card number?+

No. We never need card numbers, passwords or codes.

Do I need to share my Facebook or Meta login?+
