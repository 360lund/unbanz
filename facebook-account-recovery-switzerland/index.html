Facebook account recovery in Switzerland.

Facebook account hacked, suspended or locked and you’re in Switzerland? Here are the official steps, how Swiss reporting works, and how Unbanz helps you appeal through Meta’s official process.

Short answer

In Switzerland, Facebook account recovery follows Facebook’s own routes: Disagree with decision on a suspension notice within the time shown, or facebook.com/hacked for a hijacked account. Report phishing and fraud through the Swiss channels too. Unbanz helps you appeal through Meta’s official process; Meta makes the final decision.

Official steps that come first

  1. If you see a suspended or disabled notice, log in and follow the prompts to disagree with the decision. Facebook gives most suspended accounts 180 days, but trust the date on your own screen.
  2. If you cannot log in because details were changed, go to facebook.com/hacked on a device and browser you used before.
  3. At facebook.com/login/identify, search for your account and choose the option for no longer having access to your email or phone.
  4. Look in your inbox and spam for a Facebook message about the change, and use its undo link first.
  5. If Facebook asks for ID or a video selfie, use your real details and follow the instructions exactly.

What matters in Switzerland

  • Phishing reports: the Federal Office for Cybersecurity (BACS, formerly NCSC) takes reports at report.ncsc.admin.ch. It cannot accept criminal complaints; those go to your local police or Suisse ePolice (suisse-epolice.ch).
  • Typical bait: a message that looks like Swiss Post, your bank or TWINT, or a “your Page will be deleted” warning. Never sign in through a link you received in a message.
  • Groups and Pages: many Swiss clubs, neighbourhood groups and small shops run on Facebook. If your profile is disabled and you are the only admin, a Page or group can be left without one, so name a second admin when you can.
  • Your +41 number: write 079 as +41 79, without the zero. If the SIM is lost, ask your provider to block it and issue a new one before you start recovery.
  • Language: the notice appears in your phone’s language, in German, French or Italian. Send us a screenshot as shown.

What not to do

  • Don’t pay or message anyone from a DM who says they can “open” your account.
  • Don’t share your password or login codes with anyone, including us.
  • Don’t create new accounts to plead with Facebook, and don’t send the same appeal over and over.

How do I recover a hacked Facebook account in Switzerland?+

Go to facebook.com/hacked on a device and browser you have used before, or search for your account at facebook.com/login/identify and choose the option for no longer having access. Check the email on the account first for a message from Facebook with a link to undo a change.

What happens if I miss the 180 days on a Facebook suspension?+

Facebook says the account is then permanently disabled and you cannot request another review. The time to appeal can differ, so check the date on your own notice.

Does the EU Digital Services Act help me in Switzerland?+

Not directly. Switzerland is not in the EU, so the EU out-of-court dispute bodies are meant for users in EU countries. In Switzerland, the in-app appeal is the route that applies.
