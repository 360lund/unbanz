Can I make a new Facebook ad account after a ban?

Usually it’s a bad idea. Meta links new ad accounts to the people, cards, domains and devices behind a banned one, and treats it as “circumventing systems”, which can get your profile and whole Business Portfolio restricted.

Updated 29 October 2026

Lots of advertisers try a fresh account and lose it within days. Then they lose the portfolio and profile too, which is much harder to come back from.

What Meta links together

Admin profiles and their devices, payment cards, your website domain and pixel, and your business name and address.

Why a fresh account is risky

A new account can be disabled and take the portfolio and profile with it, which is much harder to come back from.

What to do instead

Request review of the original account, fix the cause and wait for the decision before running anything new.

What Meta links together

  • Admin profiles and their devices.
  • Payment cards and billing details.
  • Your website domain and pixel.
  • Business name, address and verification.

What to do instead

  1. Request review of the original account if you still can.
  2. Fix the cause: payment, verification, ad claims, special ad categories.
  3. Wait for the decision before running anything new.

Background on the rule: circumventing systems.

When a new account is fine

If the old one was reinstated, or Meta itself tells you to create a new one, you’re fine. Renting or buying “aged” ad accounts isn’t a fix. They get banned and linked to you.

Can I use a different card?+

Meta links more than cards. A new card alone doesn’t make it a new advertiser.

What about an agency ad account?+

Running your banned business through someone else’s account can still be circumventing.

Should I buy an aged ad account?+

No. It breaks Meta’s rules and risks everything linked to you.
