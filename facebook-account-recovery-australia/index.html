Checks for Australia

Phone formats, where to report fraud and any appeal rights differ by country. The sections below cover Australia.

Official steps that come first

  1. If you see a suspended or disabled notice, log in and follow the prompts to disagree with the decision. Facebook gives most suspended accounts 180 days, but trust the date on your own screen.
  2. If you cannot log in because details were changed, go to facebook.com/hacked on a device and browser you have used before.
  3. You can also search for your account at facebook.com/login/identify and choose the option for no longer having access to your email or phone.
  4. Check your inbox and spam for a Facebook email about the change and use the undo link if there is one.
  5. If Facebook asks for ID or a video selfie, use your real details and follow the instructions exactly. Then wait for the reply instead of filing repeat appeals.

Australian details worth knowing

  • Marketplace and group scams: a common way in is a buyer or “courier” message with a link to a fake Facebook login. Scamwatch (scamwatch.gov.au) lists current scam types and takes reports, and ReportCyber at cyber.gov.au handles cybercrime.
  • If a friend’s account messages you for money: call them on a number you already have before sending anything, and tell them to start recovery.
  • Pages and ads: if your profile is disabled, a Page where you are the only admin can be lost and linked ad accounts can stop. Ask someone you trust to confirm they have full control of the Page. For ad account problems see ad accounts disabled in Australia.
  • Your +61 number: write 04xx as +61 4xx without the zero, and get a lost SIM replaced through your provider before you start.
  • Harassment and intimate images: the eSafety Commissioner (esafety.gov.au) can act on image-based abuse and serious cyberbullying. IDCARE (idcare.org) gives free support after an account or identity compromise.

If the notice is about your age

Facebook is one of the platforms that must take reasonable steps to keep Australians under 16 off accounts since 10 December 2025. Adults who lose access by mistake are offered an age check through Yoti, with a video selfie or government ID. Use it from the notice itself. It is a separate step from an appeal about a rule violation, so tell us which notice you are looking at.

What not to do

  • Don’t pay or message anyone from a DM who says they can “open” your account.
  • Don’t share your password or login codes with anyone, including us.
  • Don’t create new accounts to plead with Facebook, and don’t send the same appeal over and over.

Good to know.

How do I recover a hacked Facebook account in Australia?+

Go to facebook.com/hacked on a device and browser you have used before, or search for your account at facebook.com/login/identify and choose the option for no longer having access. Check the email on the account first for a message from Facebook with a link to undo a change.

What happens if I miss the 180 days on a Facebook suspension?+

Facebook says the account is then permanently disabled and you cannot request another review. The time to appeal can differ, so check the date on your own notice and act early.

Facebook locked me out after the Australian age rules. Is that an appeal or an age check?+

If the notice mentions age, it is the age check through Yoti. If it names a rule violation, it is a normal appeal. Send us a screenshot and we tell you which applies.

Can Unbanz recover my account?+

We help you submit a clean appeal through Meta’s official process and handle it end to end. Meta makes the final decision, so we tell you upfront whether we think it’s doable.

Do I need to share my Facebook password?+

No. Your password and login codes stay with you. Anyone who asks for them is not someone to work with.
