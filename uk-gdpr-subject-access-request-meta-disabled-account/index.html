In the UK and your account was disabled? You can ask Meta for your data.

A subject access request will not reverse a ban, but it can show what Meta holds about the account and the decision.

Updated 29 October 2026

Under UK GDPR you can ask Meta for a copy of the personal data it holds about you, including data linked to a disabled account. Meta must normally reply within one month, which can be extended by two months for complex requests. If the reply is late or incomplete, raise it with Meta first, then complain to the ICO.

How to make the request

  1. Use Meta’s privacy contact form in the Privacy Center or Help Center, and choose data access.
  2. Give the username, the email or phone on the account, and the date it was disabled.
  3. Ask for the personal data held about the account, including records about the enforcement decision.
  4. Keep a copy and the date sent.

Automated decisions

You can also ask whether the decision was made solely by automated means. UK GDPR gives extra rights where a solely automated decision has a legal or similarly significant effect, which may apply when a business account is removed. This page is general information, not legal advice.

Complaining to the ICO

If Meta does not reply in time, or the reply is incomplete, write to Meta first saying so. If that does not work, complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office at ico.org.uk. The ICO looks at how your data was handled. It cannot order your account back.

What to send

  • Your Facebook profile link or the name on the profile.
  • A screenshot of the message or error, with the date visible.
  • Which email address or phone number is on the account (just which one, never a code).
  • What happened before it started, in a few lines.

Please do not send passwords, login codes, card numbers or access tokens.
