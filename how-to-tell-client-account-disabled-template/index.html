How to tell a client their account is disabled.

Bad news lands better when it is short, calm and clear about what happens next. Here are the words.

When a client’s account is disabled, tell them three things: what happened, what you are doing and what you cannot promise. Keep it short and calm, ask for the notice screenshot, and warn them not to try random logins. Never promise a result, because the platform decides.

A three-part script

  1. What happened: the account is disabled or restricted, and the platform sent a notice.
  2. What you are doing: starting the official recovery process and gathering the details.
  3. What you cannot promise: the platform decides, and you will report each step.

Message one: first reply

First reply

I have seen the notice. I am starting the recovery process today. Please send a screenshot of the notice and the date it appeared. Do not try different logins and do not share any codes with anyone. I will update you as soon as I have news. The platform makes the final decision, so I cannot promise an outcome.

Message two: when the client is panicking

Panicking client

I understand this is stressful. Nothing is lost yet. The best thing you can do is wait and avoid new login attempts, which can make things harder. I will handle the process and keep you updated. I cannot promise how it ends, but I will tell you everything I learn.

Message three: bad news

When the platform says no

The platform has given its decision and it has not been restored. That decision is final for this request. I am sorry. Here is what we can do next: set up a new account properly, move your content to other channels and make sure this does not happen again.

Words to avoid

  • “Definitely”, “for sure” or any promise about the result.
  • “I know someone at Meta.”
  • “It will be back by Friday.”
  • Blaming the client for what happened before you know the cause.

Good to know.

What should I say first to a client with a disabled account?+

Tell them what you are doing, ask for the screenshot and date, and tell them not to try random logins or share codes.

Should I give the client a timeframe?+

No. The platform decides the timing and the result, so any date you give may be wrong.

How do I deliver bad news?+

Be direct, say the decision is final for that request, and offer clear next steps.

Do I tell the client a partner is involved?+

That is your choice. If the client asks directly, answer honestly.

Can I copy these templates?+

Yes. Change the wording to sound like you.
