Can that Instagram username actually be claimed?

Most claims come down to four things: how long the handle is, how dead the account looks, whether it is a common word, and whether you have a brand or trademark behind it.

Updated 4 November 2026

An Instagram username can usually only be claimed when the account holding it looks inactive, the handle is not a common word, and you have a real reason to use it. Handles of six or more characters tied to your name, brand or trademark are the most realistic. Active accounts, short dictionary words and handles in a live dispute are normally ruled out. Instagram makes the final decision.

Quick checklist

  1. Open the profile. No posts, no profile photo and no recent activity is a good sign.
  2. Count the characters. Six or more is the usual range for a standard claim; shorter handles are rarer and harder.
  3. Ask whether it is a common word, first name or city. Those are treated as high-value and are much harder.
  4. Note your reason: your legal name, your business name, a registered trademark or a domain you own.
  5. Check the handle is not the subject of a dispute or tied to a public figure.

What usually rules a claim out

  • The account posted, changed its photo or added a story recently.
  • The handle belongs to an active brand or person.
  • You plan to resell the handle.
  • The only route offered involves logging into someone else's account or buying it.

What to send

  • Your profile link or username.
  • A screenshot of the message or error, with the date visible.

Username questions people also ask (2026)

Does a trademark help with a username?

It can. Instagram’s trademark form is for accounts using a registered mark in a way that confuses people. Have the registration number, the owner name and the country of registration ready, and use the same name on the request.

Is a username claim safe for my account?

A claim should never need your password or login codes. Buying or selling usernames or accounts goes against Instagram’s Terms of Use and can get both accounts removed, so avoid marketplaces.

Can I claim a username from an inactive account?

Instagram has no public form to request an inactive username. A name usually frees up only when the holding account is removed or deleted. A registered trademark adds an official route: Instagram’s trademark report form, which covers accounts that infringe the mark, not just accounts that share the name.
