Instagram hacked and now disabled, with no appeal option?

When a hacker gets the account disabled, the usual appeal screen can be missing. These are the routes that remain.

Updated 29 October 2026

Message us on WhatsApp ↗︎Prefer email? ↗︎

If a hacker triggered the disabling, the account may show a message with no appeal button because the login details were changed. The appeal path is then the hacked-account recovery route, using a video selfie and the details Instagram already holds. Start with the notice and the email on file.

Treat it as a hack first

The recovery route for hacked accounts is different from the standard appeal.

Check your email

Look for messages about changed email, phone or password. They show what was altered.

Prepare the selfie

Hacked-account recovery often asks for a video selfie, so be ready for good light.

Why there is no button

The appeal button depends on the account state and the contact details attached to it. If a hacker changed the email or phone, the screen can show only an error or a login prompt.

  • The original email may have been replaced.
  • Two-factor authentication may now point at the hacker.
  • The disabled notice may only show on certain devices.

Routes to try

  1. Search your inbox and spam for messages about a changed email or phone, and use the revert link if one is there.
  2. Use Instagram’s own help flow for a hacked account and follow the video selfie steps.
  3. Try from the device and network you used before the hack.
  4. Write to us with the notice and the dates, and we will look at what fits.

What to send

  • Your Instagram username (the public @handle).
  • A screenshot of the notice or error, with the date visible.
  • Which email address or phone number is on the account (just which one, never a code).
  • What happened in the days before it started, in a few lines.

Please do not send passwords, login codes, card numbers or access tokens.

Who this is for

  • People who found a changed email and a disabled notice.
